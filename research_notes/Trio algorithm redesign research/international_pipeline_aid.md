## Key Question 1 — CamDiab CamAPS FX (adaptive MPC), CamAPS HX, CamAPS Liberty, and the Cambridge fully-closed-loop results

### Takeaway
CamAPS FX is an adaptive model-predictive-control (MPC) hybrid closed loop that recalculates insulin every 8–12 min over a 2.5–4 h horizon, learns diurnal/day-to-day insulin needs over 1–3 weeks, and exposes Boost (+~35 % basal) and Ease-off (+45 mg/dL target) as non-learned temporary modes. The same algorithm family, run without meal input (CamAPS HX), reached TIR 66.3 % in insulin-treated T2D (vs 32.3 %) but only ~45–50 % TIR in T1D adults/adolescents with high HbA1c even with Lyumjev/Fiasp; CamDiab nevertheless obtained a notified-body (CE) approval in Jan 2026 and launched the FCL mode commercially as "CamAPS Liberty" inside CamAPS FX at ATTD (10 Mar 2026), pending country-level availability.

### Cited Findings

**Algorithm architecture and user-facing modes (CamAPS FX hybrid)**
- CamAPS FX uses model predictive control; it "calculates the insulin requirement for the next 2.5-4 hours, and insulin delivery is adjusted every 8-12 minutes"; "the adaptive model learns from previous insulin delivery and meal bolus information to adapt to diurnal, day-to-day, and post-meal insulin needs" — [Diabetesnet overview of CamAPS FX](https://www.diabetesnet.com/?p=8550); same description on the [mylife Loop product page](https://www.mylife-diabetescare.com/loop).
- The system "learns insulin sensitivity and takes on average 1-3 weeks to optimize"; during this period users are asked to review carb ratios and the personal glucose target — [Diabetesnet](https://www.diabetesnet.com/?p=8550).
- Boost "makes the algorithm more 'responsive' and increases basal insulin delivery by ~35%"; Ease-off "reduces the amount of insulin delivered ... When it is active, the algorithm increases the target glucose level" by 45 mg/dL (2.5 mmol/L) above the personal target — [German AID Steckbrief CamAPS FX, Feb 2026 (diabetes-technologie.de)](https://diabetes-technologie.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Steckbrief_AID_CamAPS-FX_final_022026.pdf).
- Ease-off and Boost "support temporary changes in insulin needs, so this information does not feed into the algorithm's learning"; advice is not to use them in the first 2–3 weeks "so as not to prolong the algorithm's learning phase" — [same Steckbrief](https://diabetes-technologie.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Steckbrief_AID_CamAPS-FX_final_022026.pdf).
- Boost is advised "for times when your glucose is unusually high, such as during illness and stress" — [Dutch dietitian HCL supplement (nvdietist.nl)](https://nvdietist.nl/app/uploads/2025/02/Aanvulling-hybride-closed-loop-systeem-samenva.pdf).
- Initialization inputs for the Cambridge controller (as implemented in the HX trial): "Initialized from total daily insulin dose and body weight. Default glucose target is 5.8 mmol/L, adjustable from 4.4 to 11.0 mmol/L"; new infusion rate every 8–12 min; Android phone sends rates to the pump — [Daly et al., Nat Med 2023, PMC9873557](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9873557/).
- Hybrid-mode insulin comparison (CamAPS FX, 28 adults, two 8-week periods): TIR 78.7 ± 9.8 % with ultra-rapid lispro vs 76.2 ± 9.6 % with standard lispro, difference 2.5 pts (95 % CI 0.8–4.2; p = 0.005) — [Nwokolo/Lakshman et al., DTT 2023 (boris.unibe.ch PDF)](https://boris.unibe.ch/187146/1/dia.2023.0262.pdf). [peer-reviewed]

**Pregnancy and children (hybrid CamAPS FX)**
- AiDAPT (NEJM 2023): 124 pregnant participants with T1D randomized to CamAPS FX vs standard care from 16 weeks; primary outcome time in pregnancy range 3.5–7.8 mmol/L; HCL group "10.5 percentage points higher"; secondary outcomes favored HCL on mean glucose, TAR, overnight TIR and HbA1c — [Healio, Mar 2024](https://www.healio.com/news/endocrinology/20240322/hybrid-closedloop-system-boosts-time-in-range-for-pregnant-women-with-type-1-diabetes). [peer-reviewed]
- FDA cleared CamAPS FX as the first HCL algorithm for use during pregnancy in the US (July 2024) — [Healio, Jul 2024](https://www.healio.com/news/endocrinology/20240715/fda-oks-first-artificial-pancreas-for-managing-type-1-diabetes-during-pregnancy). [approved]
- A 2026 Diabetic Medicine paper reports HbA1c improvement in pregnant women using CamAPS FX in UK real-world use — [Syleouni et al., Diabetic Medicine 2026 (White Rose eprint)](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/242907/1/Diabetic%20Medicine%20-%202026%20-%20Syleouni%20-%20HbA1c%20improvement%20in%20pregnant%20women%20with%20type%201%20diabetes%20using%20the%20CamAPS%20FX.pdf). [peer-reviewed]
- CamAPS FX is FDA cleared but "not yet available in the U.S."; the Cambridge pediatric 6-month multicentre RCT of the Cambridge HCL algorithm in children/adolescents is published — [DiabetesTimes](https://diabetestimes.co.uk/automated-fully-closed-loop-insulin-delivery-improves-type-2-diabetes-outcomes-research-finds/); [Ware et al. RCT listing (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/359090245_Cambridge_hybrid_closed-loop_algorithm_in_children_and_adolescents_with_type_1_diabetes_a_multicentre_6-month_randomised_controlled_trial).

**Fully closed loop: CamAPS HX in type 2 diabetes (Daly et al., Nature Medicine, 12 Jan 2023) [peer-reviewed]**
- Randomized open-label single-center two-period crossover; 30 recruited, 28 randomized, 26 analysed; two 8-week periods, 2–4 week washout; CL arm used Fiasp via Dana Diabecare pump + Dexcom G6, "delivered continuously without prandial boluses or carbohydrate announcement"; control = usual MDI with masked G6 — [PMC9873557](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9873557/).
- TIR 66.3 ± 14.9 % vs 32.3 ± 24.7 % (difference 35.3 pts, 95 % CI 28.0–42.6, P < 0.001); TAR 33.2 % vs 67.0 %; TBR < 3.9 median 0.44 % vs 0.08 % (NS, P = 0.43); TBR < 3.0 0.04 % vs 0.03 %; mean glucose 9.2 vs 12.6 mmol/L; HbA1c 57 ± 9 (7.3 %) vs 72 ± 13 mmol/mol (8.7 %); TDD 108 U/day (0.90 U/kg) vs 84 U/day; no severe hypoglycaemia; 4 SAEs on CL (1 study-related cannula-site abscess) — [PMC9873557](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9873557/); summary also at [Cambridge BRC](https://cambridgebrc.nihr.ac.uk/2023/01/12/fully-automated-closed-loop-insulin-delivery-in-adults-with-type-2-diabetes-an-open-label-single-center-randomized-crossover-trial/).
- Earlier dialysis trial (Nat Med 2021, 26 adults with T2D on dialysis): TIR 52.8 ± 12.5 % CL vs 37.7 ± 20.5 % control — [PMC8363503](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8363503/). [peer-reviewed]
- Follow-on 26-week home RCT "COYOTE" (NCT06579404) planned 224 participants (112/arm), HbA1c primary endpoint, CamAPS HX vs standard insulin therapy + CGM in insulin-treated T2D — [ICH-GCP registry mirror](https://ichgcp.net/clinical-trials-registry/NCT06579404). [registry]
- Inpatient FCL real-world implementation outcomes published (PubMed 36947090) — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36947090). [peer-reviewed; numbers not extracted]

**Fully closed loop: CamAPS HX in type 1 diabetes [peer-reviewed]**
- CLEAR (NCT04977908; Boughton et al., Diabetes Care 2023): single-center randomized crossover, 26 adults with T1D and suboptimal control (HbA1c ≈ 9.2 %), CamAPS HX + ultra-rapid lispro (Lyumjev) with Dana pump + Dexcom G6 vs pump+CGM, two 8-week periods — [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT04977908](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04977908); [Diabetesgeneeskunde summary](https://arts.diabetesgeneeskunde.nl/?p=52999).
- Pooled retrospective analysis of the T1D (n = 26, Lyumjev) and T2D (n = 26, Fiasp) FCL periods: TIR 49.9 ± 9.6 % (T1D) vs 66.3 ± 14.9 % (T2D); TBR < 70 median 0.86 % vs 0.43 %; TBR < 54 0.12 % vs 0.04 %; TAR > 180 49.1 ± 9.9 % vs 33.2 ± 14.8 %; > 300 mg/dL 9.3 % vs 1.8 %. T1D hypoglycaemia peaked 08:00–14:00 and 18:00–20:00; T1D > 300 mg/dL peaked 21:00–22:00; TIR highest 05:00–09:00 in both. Authors "suggest that daytime meal announcements may be important for type 1 users to reach target time in range" — [PMC11571715](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11571715/).
- A Boughton conference slide reports a 12-percentage-point TIR gain for FCL in that adult cohort but lists n = 36 (conflicts with the 26 in the registry) — [ABCD slide deck](https://abcd.care/sites/default/files/1500%20-%20Charlotte%20Boughton.pdf). [conference; conflict noted]
- Adolescents (Kadiyala et al., DTT 2025; CLEAR Phase 2, NCT05653050): two-center randomized crossover, 24 adolescents aged 13–19 with HbA1c ≥ 7.5 %, CamAPS HX with Fiasp vs pump + CGM; TIR ≈ 45 % FCL vs ≈ 32 % control (significant); HbA1c did not differ — [PubMed 40445776](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40445776/); [DOI 10.1089/DIA.2025.0062](https://doi.org/10.1089/DIA.2025.0062); [Medscape 2025](https://www.medscape.com/viewarticle/how-close-fully-closed-loop-automated-insulin-delivery-2025a1000862). Lived-experience sub-study: all interviewees reported less effort and less worry — [Diabetes Res Clin Pract 2025](https://www.diabetesresearchclinicalpractice.com/article/S0168-8227(25)01093-9/fulltext).
- Lyumjev vs standard lispro under FCL with simulated missed meal boluses (18 adults, two 8-h inpatient sessions, CamAPS FX run without bolus): TIR 49.3 ± 15.6 % vs 39.9 ± 18.9 %, p = 0.072 (NS) — [PMC12434446](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12434446/). [peer-reviewed]
- CLOSE-IT (Breakthrough T1D-funded, published 2026, "largest study to date on FCL effectiveness in adults with T1D"): "time in range for FCL and HCL were similar"; FCL safe with favourable outcomes for most — [Breakthrough T1D UK, "CamAPS Liberty: behind the headlines"](https://breakthrought1d.org.uk/news/camaps-liberty-behind-the-headlines/). [secondary summary; primary numbers not retrieved]

**Regulatory / commercial: CE approval (Jan 2026) and CamAPS Liberty (Mar 2026)**
- 9 Jan 2026: CamDiab announced European (notified-body) approval of its fully closed-loop algorithm for T1D "without carb counting or pre-meal insulin dosing"; "pilot is planned for early 2026, with a wider rollout to follow"; pumps from TecMed (formerly Ypsomed Diabetes Care); CGMs FreeStyle Libre 3 / 3 Plus and Dexcom G6 (iOS); no clinical numbers in the release; Hovorka: approval "brings fully closed-loop delivery into routine type 1 diabetes care" — [Drug Delivery Business, 9 Jan 2026](https://www.drugdeliverybusiness.com/camdiab-ce-mark-fully-closed-loop-insulin-algorithm/). [approved — CE]
- 10 Mar 2026 (ATTD Barcelona): CamAPS Liberty unveiled as a fully closed-loop mode inside the CamAPS FX app, "granted approval by CamDiab's notified body in January 2026"; runs "a more proactive version of the algorithm that anticipates and responds to glucose changes while maintaining established safety boundaries"; Boost, Ease-off, Add Meal and manual boluses remain available; "not been studied in pregnancy or in children under 13 years of age and is therefore not recommended"; availability "pending market-specific approvals" — [MassDevice](https://www.massdevice.com/camdiab-unveils-fully-closed-loop-insulin-feature/); [Drug Delivery Business](https://www.drugdeliverybusiness.com/camdiab-unveils-fully-closed-loop-insulin-feature/); [mylife Diabetes Care release](https://www.mylife-diabetescare.com/en/community/media-releases/media-release-detail/mylife-diabetes-care-and-camdiab-introduce-camaps-liberty-the-first-fully-closed-loop-option-within-myloop); [CamDiab press release](https://camdiab.com/press-releases/liberty-260310); [DiabetesTimes](https://diabetestimes.co.uk/camaps-liberty-the-dawn-of-fully-closed-loop-technology/). [announced/approved]
- Liberty can be switched on and off with no built-in time limit; first-use training video, a block feature against accidental activation, and Companion remote monitoring are included; Breakthrough T1D says more trial data are needed before FCL is used "constantly rather than temporarily" — [Breakthrough T1D UK](https://breakthrought1d.org.uk/news/camaps-liberty-behind-the-headlines/).
- CamAPS HX for T2D exists as a separate Android app listing — [Google Play, CamAPS HX for T2D](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.camdiab.fx_alert.hx.mmoll&hl=en_US).

### Inferences
- The Cambridge FCL evidence shows a strong diabetes-type effect: residual endogenous insulin in T2D makes unannounced-meal control easy (TIR 66 %, TBR < 0.5 %), while in C-peptide-negative T1D the same MPC with the fastest available analogs plateaus at ~45–50 % TIR in poorly controlled cohorts, with post-dinner hyperglycaemia the dominant failure mode. The commercial Liberty mode is therefore positioned as an optional, switchable "no-bolus" mode rather than a replacement for hybrid operation; CLOSE-IT's "similar TIR" suggests that in well-controlled adults the proactive MPC can roughly match HCL.
- CamAPS's design choice of keeping Boost/Ease-off out of the learning loop and "freezing" learning in the first 2–3 weeks is a practical rule a redesign could borrow: temporary user overrides should not contaminate long-horizon parameter adaptation.

### Gaps
- Published CLOSE-IT numbers (n, TIR, TBR) were not retrieved; only Breakthrough T1D's summary was accessible.
- Exact TIR for the comparator arm of CLEAR (adult T1D) is not in the sources retrieved; only the FCL arm (49.9 %) and a slide claiming +12 pts.
- Specific KidsAP02 very-young-children NEJM numbers and the exact AiDAPT TIR percentages (commonly quoted as 68.2 % vs 55.6 %) could not be verified from retrieved pages; only the +10.5-pt difference is sourced.
- No public description of HX/Liberty's internal meal-handling (e.g., glucose-rise–triggered insulin acceleration) beyond "proactive MPC" and 8–12-min recalculation; CamDiab's patents were not reviewed.
- No launch-country list or US (FDA) status for Liberty as of Oct 2026 was found.

## Key Question 2 — Diabeloop DBLG1 / DBL-hu / DBLG2 (expert system + ML), meal categories, no-carb-count roadmap, financial status

### Takeaway
DBLG1 is a smartphone-hosted "machine-learning-based" expert-system controller initialized with weight, TDD and typical meal size, accepting simplified meal labels (small/medium/large); its pivotal 12-week crossover gave TIR 68.5 % vs 59.4 %, and 12-month real-world data (n = 4,162) show +17.6 pts TIR. DBLG2 (CE-MDR Aug 2025; FDA 510(k) iAGC 18 Dec 2025, T1D ≥ 12 y) makes meal announcements optional with a self-learning parameter module, at a reported cost of 3–6 TIR points when meals are not announced; Diabeloop reports it met 2025 sales targets but undertook a "major balance sheet restructuring" in 2025 and is pivoting to a US partner model (Sequel twiist, 2027).

### Cited Findings

**DBLG1 algorithm and inputs**
- Pivotal trial (Benhamou et al., Lancet Digital Health 2019, NCT02987556): multicentre open-label randomized crossover; 68 randomized, 63 analysed; 2-week run-in, 12 weeks per arm, 8-week washout; "DBLG1 is a hybrid closed-loop system using a machine-learning-based algorithm"; TIR 68.5 ± 9.4 % vs 59.4 ± 10.2 % with sensor-augmented pump (difference 9.2 pts, 95 % CI 6.4–11.9, p < 0.0001); severe hypoglycaemia 5 vs 3 episodes "linked to hardware malfunctions or human error" — [Europe PMC record / DOI 10.1016/S2589-7500(19)30003-2](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2589-7500(19)30003-2). [peer-reviewed]
- At startup users input "their total daily insulin dose, typical meal size in grams of carbs, and body weight" — [diaTribe](https://diatribe.org/diabetes-technology/diabeloops-aid-system-improves-time-range-and-quality-life).
- Meals can be declared with simplified labels; in 12-month real-world data "about half of the meals users logged were entered as small, medium, or large labels"; users declared one fewer meal per day on average after ~6 months — [Newswire, 12-month real-world achievements](https://www.newswire.com/news/12-month-real-world-achievements-for-diabeloops-aid-21827985); [Fierce Biotech](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/medtech/diabeloops-insulin-delivery-ai-boosts-time-range-minimizes-hypoglycemia-one-year-data). [company-reported]
- Real world: 4,162 patients across five European countries, TIR improved 17.6 pts over one year; Benhamou: "TIR>70%, TBR<4%, GMI<7% can be expected in about half of patients starting DBLG1" — [Newswire](https://www.newswire.com/news/12-month-real-world-achievements-for-diabeloops-aid-21827985). [company-reported]
- Adolescent sub-analysis (ESPE 2021, 37 adolescents, NCT04190277, 668 patient-days): no correlation between TIR and daily carbohydrate intake (r = −0.05); "performance was not altered by daily meal-size variation" — [ESPE e-poster](https://abstracts.eurospe.org/hrp/0094/eposters/hrp0094p2-128_eposter.pdf). [conference]
- Roche/Diabeloop (2020) integration on the Accu-Chek Insight pump: "the Diabeloop algorithm can stop insulin delivery, adjust the basal rate or deliver automatic correction bolus" — [MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/roche-inks-deal-diabeloop-integrate-automated-insulin-delivery).
- French reimbursement approved 2021; device dispensed via pharmacies — [CEA start-up page](https://cea.fr/english/Pages/innovation/start-ups/diabeloop.aspx); [Le Quotidien du Médecin](https://cancerologie.lequotidiendumedecin.fr/specialites/diabetologie-endocrinologie/pancreas-artificiel-desormais-rembourse-le-dispositif-diabeloop-va-etre-delivre-par-les-officines).

**DBL-hu (highly unstable T1D)**
- NCT04042207 (2019–2021): adults ≥ 22 y, T1D ≥ 5 y, C-peptide negative; N-of-1 randomized design vs predictive low-glucose suspend; 7 randomized, 5 completed; TIR 73.3 ± 1.7 % (DBL-hu) vs 43.5 ± 1.7 % (PLGS); benefits maintained at 6 months in an extension (Diabetes Obes Metab 2022); CE marked — [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT04042207](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04042207); [HAL record hal-04556428](https://hal.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/hal-04556428); [Diabeloop press release](https://www.diabeloop.com/media-press/press-releases/diabeloop-introduces-an-automated-management-solution-for-highly-unstable-diabetes). [peer-reviewed / approved CE]

**DBLG2 (no mandatory meal announcement)**

- 23 May 2026: Diabeloop announced CE marking and gradual European launch of DBLG2 integrated with Dexcom G7 + Kaleido — [OpenPR](https://www.openpr.com/news/4524744/diabeloop-announces-ce-marking-and-gradual-launch-of-the-new); CEO page notes a 10 Jul 2026 item "DBLG2 launch with the Kaleido patch pump and Dexcom G7 CGM, open for prescription" — [Diabeloop CEO reflection](https://www.diabeloop.com/news/company/our-ceos-reflection-on-diabeloops-2025-and-looking-ahead-to-2026-for-advancing-diabetes-care-together). [approved/launched EU]

### Inferences
- Diabeloop's "no carb count" path is incremental: the controller was always tolerant of coarse meal labels (performance insensitive to meal-size variation), and DBLG2 formalizes meal-optional operation with a self-learning parameter module; the 3–6-pt TIR penalty is the best quantified cost of dropping announcements in a commercially cleared system.
- The 2025 "balance sheet restructuring" plus loss of the Roche Insight platform and the pivot to third-party pumps (Kaleido, Dana-i, twiist, Terumo) implies Diabeloop is a capital-constrained algorithm licensor rather than a vertically integrated vendor.

## Key Question 3 — Inreda AP (bihormonal insulin + glucagon, fully closed loop, Netherlands)

### Takeaway
Inreda AP is a CE-marked (Feb 2020) dual-chamber, fully automatic insulin+glucagon system with no meal or exercise input; it reports the highest FCL TIR of any system here — median 86.6 % in a 2-week crossover and mean 80.3 % (from 55.5 %) sustained over 12 months in 71 Dutch real-world users, with TBR ~1.4 % — at the cost of two infusion sets, glucagon logistics, device size, and a small set of SAEs; Dutch basic-insurance reimbursement hinges on a 240-patient, 2-year UMC Utrecht study, and a new algorithm version is in validation.

### Cited Findings
- Diabetes Care 2021 outpatient randomized crossover in adults with T1D: median TIR 86.6 % closed loop vs 53.9 % open loop (P < 0.0001), with lower hypo- and hyperglycaemia time — [Diabetes Care 44(3):836](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/44/3/836/138610/Fully-Closed-Loop-Glucose-Control-With-a); [Healio](https://www.healio.com/news/endocrinology/20210211/bihormonal-artificial-pancreas-system-improves-time-in-range-reduces-hypoglycemia). [peer-reviewed]

## Key Question 4 — Luna Diabetes (overnight-only automated insulin for MDI users)

### Takeaway
Luna is an investigational, sleep-only patch pump (≤ 10 U fill, applied at bedtime, removed in the morning) for people on basal/bolus pens: it keeps the user's long-acting basal, connects to the user's CGM, and gives algorithm-driven rapid-acting micro-boluses overnight with no basal-rate/ISF/ICR programming (only the daily basal dose is entered). Feasibility data show ~+10 % TIR and −15 % time > 250 mg/dL without more hypoglycaemia; a 120–130-participant, 13-week pivotal (AID4MDI, NCT06627517; TIR primary) started Nov 2024 and was expected to complete early 2026, but no topline results or FDA clearance were found as of Oct 2026.

### Cited Findings
- Setup: "You enter your total daily basal insulin dose in the app and connect Luna to your CGM"; "no carb ratios, basal rates, insulin sensitivity factors, or insulin action times are needed"; overnight micro-boluses of rapid-acting insulin from real-time CGM; no daytime device ("Transitionless AID"); includes a "Drift Detection" safety feature; works "alongside your long-acting (basal) insulin"; labeled "CAUTION—Investigational device" — [Luna product page](https://www.lunadiabetes.com/patients/product). [company-reported]
- Pod is filled with up to 10 units, applied before bed, removed in the morning; "integrates with any continuous glucose monitor"; algorithm "adapts automatically without user-programmed settings"; founders say Luna "is not designed to match the full clinical performance of traditional AID systems" but to expand access/reduce burden; possible T2D/GLP-1 and primary-care use; pivotal: 120 participants with T1D, 13 weeks, safety/efficacy/sleep-quality PROs; feasibility: average +10 % TIR, −15 % time > 250 mg/dL, no increase in hypoglycaemia — [HCPLive podcast with Jon Brilliant and John Sjölund, 13 May 2025](https://www.hcplive.com/view/diabetes-dialogue-luna-automated-insulin-delivery-with-jon-brilliant-and-john-sj-lund). [company-reported]
- Pivotal registry NCT06627517 (AID4MDI): adults with T1D on MDI; actual start Nov 2024; estimated primary completion Sept 2025; primary endpoint TIR 70–180; secondary HbA1c and Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index; up to 130 participants; device worn 13 weeks within a 17-week study — [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT06627517](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06627517); [Clinical Trials Arena](https://www.clinicaltrialsarena.com/news/luna-diabetes-trials-automated-wearable-insulin-pump-2/); [Medical Device Network](https://www.medicaldevice-network.com/news/luna-diabetes-trials-automated-wearable-insulin-pump/). [registry]
- Company blog (22 Oct 2024): pivotal trial begun; "the results will move Luna closer to regulatory approval"; only named endpoint "Time in Range Improvement, measured by a connected CGM" — [Luna blog](https://blog.lunadiabetes.com/pivotal).

### Inferences
- Because the user's own long-acting basal covers baseline needs, Luna's controller only has to add insulin (micro-boluses) on top of a pharmacologic basal — effectively a "positive-only" overnight correction controller with a hypoglycaemia guard ("drift detection"), which avoids the hardest FCL problems (daytime meals) by design.
- The 10-U fill and overnight window bound worst-case overdose, which likely underpins the safety argument for an MDI population.

### Gaps
- No published pivotal results, no FDA submission/clearance date, no description of the control law (MPC vs rule-based), no explicit list of supported CGMs, and no pricing were found as of Oct 2026.
- Feasibility study n, duration and TBR are not reported in any retrieved source.

## Key Question 6 — Cross-cutting: techniques to go beyond user-entered carbs, and which FCL results are best

### Takeaway
Across systems, four levers are used to go beyond carb entry: (1) proactive MPC that reacts to CGM rise/acceleration with no meal model (Cambridge HX/Liberty, Tandem's planned FCL), (2) coarse meal signals (Diabeloop S/M/L labels, Medtrum meal-type Auto Meal, CamAPS "Add Meal"), (3) long-horizon self-learning of basal/sensitivity parameters (CamAPS 1–3-week learning, DBLG2 self-learning module, Medtrum APGO), and (4) pharmacology — ultra-rapid insulin (Lyumjev/Fiasp, worth only ~2.5 pts in HCL and a non-significant ~9 pts in an FCL missed-bolus test) or a second hormone (Inreda glucagon). The best demonstrated FCL results are Inreda's bihormonal ~80–87 % TIR in T1D and CamAPS HX's 66 % in T2D; insulin-only FCL in poorly controlled T1D reaches ~45–50 %, with DBLG2's meal-optional mode costing 3–6 TIR points versus full announcement.

### Cited Findings
- Insulin-only FCL in T1D (CamAPS HX + Lyumjev, 26 adults, 8 weeks): TIR 49.9 %, TAR > 300 9.3 %, hypos peaking mid-morning/early evening; authors suggest daytime meal announcements may be needed in T1D — [PMC11571715](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11571715/). Adolescents (Fiasp): ~45 % vs ~32 % — [PubMed 40445776](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40445776/).
- Insulin-only FCL in T2D (Fiasp): TIR 66.3 % vs 32.3 % — [PMC9873557](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9873557/).

### Inferences
- Ranking by demonstrated FCL TIR in T1D: Inreda bihormonal (≈80–87 %) > CLOSE-IT-type well-controlled adults on CamAPS FCL (≈ HCL, numbers not retrieved) > DBLG2 meal-optional (HCL minus 3–6 pts) > CamAPS HX in high-HbA1c T1D (45–50 %). In T2D, insulin-only FCL (66 %) already clears the 70 % consensus target for many.
- For an insulin-only algorithm redesign, the evidence suggests the highest-yield elements are: a proactive rise/acceleration-driven correction path that does not wait for a meal flag, a self-learning sensitivity/basal estimator that ignores temporary user overrides, and an optional coarse meal signal (type or size class) rather than grams — with ultra-rapid analog support as a modest additive gain.

### Gaps
- No head-to-head trial compares the different FCL approaches; all comparisons above are cross-study with different populations and baselines.
- No retrieved source quantifies the glucose-slope/acceleration thresholds or detection logic used by any commercial FCL controller; patent review would be required.
- Published CLOSE-IT and AIDANET numbers, and any ATTD 2026 abstract-level TIR for CamAPS Liberty, were not accessible.
