## Key Question 1 — How does the MiniMed 780G SmartGuard (AHCL) algorithm work?

### Takeaway
SmartGuard is a PID-type controller with two 5-minute outputs: auto-basal "microboluses" and auto-correction boluses that fire when auto-basal is already at its ceiling and sensor glucose (SG) is >120 mg/dL. It personalises its gains from a trailing 2–6-day window of total daily insulin plus estimates of fasting glucose and plasma insulin; the user still enters carbs (meal boluses via Bolus Wizard), chooses a basal target (100/110/120 mg/dL) and active insulin time (2–8 h), and the system cannot have its basal, ISF or 120 mg/dL correction target edited by the user.

### Cited Findings

**Control structure**
- "In MM780G, basal insulin administration is determined by a PID (proportional-integral-derivative) algorithm and has 2 components: microboluses and autocorrection boluses (AB), both administered every 5 min. In situations in which basal insulin needs increase, leading to glucose levels > 120 mg/dL despite maximum basal doses, the algorithm administers AB, calculated based on algorithm control parameters rather than programmed sensitivity factors. Over time, the algorithm adjusts the sensitivity factor based on previous data; this learning process uses up 6 six days of total daily insulin doses and estimates of fasting glucose and plasma insulin concentration." **[peer-reviewed, 2024]** — [Enciso Izquierdo et al., Endocrinol Diabetes Nutr 2024;71:229-235](https://www.elsevier.es/en-revista-endocrinologia-diabetes-nutricion-english-ed--413-pdf-download-S2530018024000702)
- The same paper: "Although the DIA in the MiniMed 780G system is considered in calculating the bolus, the algorithm modifies it to administer the most effective and safe final amount, based on the hypoglycemia risk in the upcoming hours." — [Enciso Izquierdo 2024](https://www.elsevier.es/en-revista-endocrinologia-diabetes-nutricion-english-ed--413-pdf-download-S2530018024000702)
- A 2023 systematic review describes the 780G as implementing "a modified proportional-integral-derivative algorithm with some features of an MD-Logic artificial pancreas algorithm" and able to "deliver automatic correction boluses up to every 5 min" **[peer-reviewed review]** — [Passanisi et al., Adv Ther 2023, doi 10.1007/s13300-023-01501-6 (via Univ. Messina repository)](https://iris.unime.it/handle/11570/3287269)
- Insulin feedback (IFB) origin: Medtronic's closed-loop patents state that in an early PID study "the meal-related insulin infusion persisted above pre-meal levels for at least four hours after the meal. This result led to the introduction of insulin feedback to the algorithm", citing Weinzimer et al. 2008 and Steil et al. 2006 as the PID antecedents **[patent]** — e.g. [US 9,364,609 "Insulin on board compensation for a closed-loop insulin infusion system"](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9364609); related Medtronic closed-loop patents: [US 9,849,239 insulin limit](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9849239), [US 9,878,096 target-glucose generation](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9878096), [US 10,130,767 sensor model supervisor](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10130767), [US 9,662,445 / 9,999,728 regulating entry into closed-loop](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9999728).

**Settings and user-facing rules (German Diabetes Society AGDT fact sheet, 2022; professional reference, not Medtronic labeling) [clinician fact sheet]** — [AGDT MiniMed 780G with SmartGuard fact sheet (PDF)](https://www.diabetesnet.com/pdfs/MiniMed-780G-with-SmartGuard.pdf)
- "the algorithm calculates parameters from the total daily insulin calculated from past 2 to 6 days"; "requires entry of all conventional pump parameters to use AID mode".
- Required extra parameters: active insulin time 2–8 h; glucose target set point 100/110/120 mg/dL (5.6/6.1/6.7 mmol/L).
- Auto-correction: "correction target value is 120 mg/dl"; "if maximum basal insulin delivery (+150%) has been reached and the glucose value is >120 mg/dl ... automatic correction bolus may be given every 5 minutes"; "no autocorrection boluses are given when temporary glucose target value is set to 150 mg/dl"; "additional manual corrections may be recommended by the system if the entered glucose value is above 120 mg/dl".
- Auto-basal: "microboluses for basal insulin requirements are calculated automatically and delivered every 5 minutes".
- Safe meal bolus: "meal bolus amount is corrected upwards, when a correction bolus is calculated based on high glucose and low insulin on board"; "meal bolus amount is corrected downwards, when a risk of postprandial hypoglycaemia is predicted (safe meal bolus)"; "mealtime information is stored for future bolus adjustments".
- Exercise mode: temp target 150 mg/dL; "no auto-correction in exercise mode".
- User CAN modify in SmartGuard: I:C ratios ("biggest impact on glycaemia and time in target range"), active insulin time ("a shorter IAT makes correction boluses more aggressive ... no effect on microboluses for basal insulin requirements"). User CANNOT modify: basal insulin delivery, insulin sensitivity factor, 120 mg/dL correction target. No temp basals or combo boluses in SmartGuard.
- Automatic exits to Safe Basal / Manual Mode: "7 hours of maximum auto-basal delivery followed by 4 hours in 'Safe Basal'"; "3-6 hours of minimal auto-basal delivery followed by 4 hours in 'Safe Basal'"; "automatic continuation in 'Safe Basal' in case of missing required BG entries"; "return to 'Manual Mode' after a further 4 hours in 'Safe Basal'"; also exits on loss of CGM data / sensor-integrity concerns. "BG required" prompts a fingerstick entry (distinct from calibration).
- Labeling constraints: approved for Humalog U100 and NovoRapid U100; age 7+; TDD 8–250 U/day.
- Current US labeling (Feb 2026 Medtronic clinical summary): "Do not use the SmartGuard feature for people who require less than 8 units or more than 250 units of total daily insulin per day"; indication is T1D ≥7 y and insulin-requiring T2D ≥18 y; works with Simplera Sync, Guardian 4 and the Instinct iCGM — [MiniMed 780G clinical evidence summary (100K RWE), US-DBA-2600236, Feb 2026](https://www.professional.minimed.com/content/dam/minimed-professional/documents/clinical-summaries/US-DBA-2600236%20MiniMed%20780G%20Clinical%20Evidence%20Summary%20100K%20RWE.pdf.coredownload.inline.pdf)

**Auto-correction frequency / prediction (company and secondary)**
- Can make "up to 12 autocorrections per hour"; before each adjustment the system considers IOB, target, rate of change and predicted future glucose; "If the system predicts a low, it subtracts insulin from a correction bolus"; users "are still expected to bolus before meals" **[secondary, quoting Medtronic's Jennifer McVean]** — [T1D Exchange, 780G features](https://t1dexchange.org/medtronic-780g-hybrid-insulin-pump-features/)
- Medtronic HCP brochure: automatic bolus "up to every 5 minutes" when max auto-basal reached and SG >120 mg/dL; auto-correction target 120 mg/dL; basal target 100/110/120 **[company]** — [Medtronic HCP brochure (mg/dL)](https://resources.cloud.medtronic-diabetes.com/sites/prd/files/documents/2022-10/hcp_brochure_mgdl_new_brand.pdf)
- FDA approval press release (21 Apr 2023) markets the 780G as the "World's First Insulin Pump with Meal Detection Technology Featuring 5-Minute Auto Corrections" **[company]** — [Medtronic, 21 Apr 2023](https://news.medtronic.com/2023-04-21-FDA-Approves-Medtronic-MiniMed-TM-780G-System-Worlds-First-Insulin-Pump-with-Meal-Detection-Technology-Featuring-5-Minute-Auto-Corrections?asPDF=1)
- Wikipedia states the algorithm "updates itself to the individual user every night at midnight" **[secondary, unverified]** — [Wikipedia, MiniMed 780G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MiniMed_780G)

**How much work the auto-correction does (real-world, Spain, 2023 data) [peer-reviewed]** — [Enciso Izquierdo 2024](https://www.elsevier.es/en-revista-endocrinologia-diabetes-nutricion-english-ed--413-pdf-download-S2530018024000702)
- n=235 users, 14-day CareLink downloads, Apr–Aug 2023, sensor use ≥95%. Mean TDD 45.1 U; basal 40.1%, prandial bolus 42.8%, auto-correction 17.3% (range 3.4–41.3%) of TDD. Cohort TIR 75.5%, TAR 22.5%, TAR>250 4.2%, TBR 2.0%, TBR<54 0.4%, GMI 6.82%.
- AB share was higher with 2-h AIT (36.1% ± 13.2%) than with 2.25–4 h (26.4% ± 13.2%), p<0.001; glucose target had no effect on AB share.
- Users with <3 meal boluses/day received more AB (46.9% vs 27.5% of TDD) and had worse control (GMI 7.12%, TIR 67.5% vs GMI 6.78%, TIR 76.5%), "However, the 2-h AIT group presented similar TAR, TIR and GMI regardless of the number of meal boluses."

### Inferences
- The "meal detection" marketing term for the 780G refers to the effect of the PID-with-correction logic reacting to an unannounced rise (SG >120 with basal saturated), not to a separate classifier; the dedicated real-time meal classifier appears only in a 2021-priority patent (see KQ5) and the Vivera algorithm.
- Because the auto-correction size derives from algorithm-learned parameters, not the user's ISF, and the AIT setting changes correction aggressiveness but not microbolus basal, the practical "tuning knobs" are target (100 vs 120), AIT (2 h recommended) and I:C ratios — which is why the "recommended optimal settings" (ROS = 100 mg/dL + 2 h AIT) show up as the key covariate in every real-world dataset in KQ2.
- The exit rules (7 h at max basal, 3–6 h at min basal) are effectively integrator anti-windup/safety caps — a design pattern directly relevant to any algorithm redesign that wants bounded autonomy.

### Gaps
- No public primary source gives the PID gains, the insulin-feedback gain, the maximum auto-basal multiplier (AGDT fact sheet says "+150%" but does not define the base), the auto-correction sizing formula, or the per-correction maximum. Medtronic has not published the AHCL design paper in the open literature; descriptions are from clinician fact sheets, patents and company brochures.
- Whether adaptation occurs once nightly (Wikipedia claim) vs continuously could not be verified against a primary source.

## Key Question 2 — 780G clinical evidence: pivotal trial, ADAPT, real-world CareLink data, optimal settings, missed boluses, T2D, preschoolers, sensor comparisons

### Takeaway
Across the pivotal trial (TIR 74.5%, TBR 2.3%), ADAPT (HbA1c −1.4 pts vs MDI+isCGM) and >100,000-user real-world cohorts (TIR ~72%, TBR ~2%), the consistent pattern is: overall TIR in the low-to-mid 70s and TIR 78–82% when users keep the 100 mg/dL target + 2 h AIT; on days with zero user boluses the system still delivered 70.9% TIR (76.3% with ROS).

### Cited Findings

**Pivotal trial (US, single-arm, NCT03959423) [peer-reviewed]** — [Carlson et al., Diabetes Technol Ther 2022;24(3):178-189 (PubMed 34694909, via NCBI E-utilities)](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&id=40607635,34694909,38113453,40955468&rettype=abstract&retmode=text)
- n=157 ITT (39 adolescents 14–21 y; 118 adults 22–75 y); ~14-day run-in then ~90-day study; glucose target 100 or 120 mg/dL each for ~45 days; 2-h AIT also evaluated.
- TIR 68.8% ± 10.5% (run-in) → 74.5% ± 6.9%; with the 100 mg/dL target 75.4%. TBR 3.3% → 2.3%. HbA1c 7.5% ± 0.8% → 7.0% ± 0.5% (n=155). Time in closed loop 94.9% ± 5.4%, 1.2 ± 0.8 exits/week. No severe hypoglycemia or DKA. Insulin type not specified in abstract.

### Inferences
- The ~5–7-point TIR gap between "all users" and "ROS users" recurs in every cohort (72.3→78.8; 78.4→81.9; 69.3→76.3 on no-bolus days), so the algorithm's attainable ceiling with a fixed 100 mg/dL target and 2-h AIT is ~79–82% TIR with ~1–2% TBR in engaged adults, and the real-world median is pulled down by conservative settings rather than by the controller.
- The no-bolus-day result (70.9% TIR; 76.3% with ROS) is the best quantitative evidence of how much a PID-plus-5-minute-corrections design recovers from fully unannounced meals; it is retrospective and selection-biased (users who stopped bolusing) and Medtronic explicitly labels it off-label.
- T2D outcomes (84.9% TIR, 0.3% TBR, 2/3 of insulin automated) suggest the same controller is more "fully closed" in practice in T2D, which is why MiniMed guides Vivera's T2D goal as "possibly higher" than 70% (KQ3).

### Gaps
- The Lancet D&E ADAPT paper itself was not retrieved; CIs and safety details come from company/secondary summaries.
- TBR on no-bolus days was not reported in the materials retrieved.
- No independent (non-Medtronic-funded) RCT of the 780G against another commercial AID was found in this search.

## Key Question 3 — The next-generation "fully closed loop" algorithm: Vivera / NMX-AID — what is announced, what is published, results, timeline

### Takeaway
MiniMed's third-generation algorithm is branded **Vivera** (development code NMX-AID / "NMX8-AID"; runs on the new 8-series "Flex" pump). Public data are feasibility-only and company-reported: ~74% TIR in 14 adults and ~65% in 13 children with no meal announcement (ATTD, March 2026); the 389-participant US pivotal GATEWAY (NCT07228117; three arms: no meal bolus / bolus every meal / bolus at will; 90 days) finished enrolling in August 2026 with primary completion estimated January 2027, and MiniMed targets US launch in 2H CY2027 subject to FDA clearance. No peer-reviewed Vivera outcome paper exists as of Oct 2026, and the ">80% TIR with no carb entry" claim is not supported by any disclosed Vivera result.

## Key Question 5 — Mechanisms for detecting unannounced meals and dosing safely; patents and papers from Medtronic authors

### Takeaway
The only detailed public description of Medtronic's unannounced-meal mechanism is patent US 11,806,137 B2 (Roy, Grosman, Parikh; priority 1 Dec 2021; granted 7 Nov 2023): a logistic-regression or LSTM classifier over a trailing 3-hour window of SG paired with model-estimated plasma insulin flags "glycemic response to a meal," after which the controller temporarily lowers the correction target and/or reduces ISF so that a series of 5-minute auto-corrections replaces the meal bolus, and any late user bolus is debited by the correction insulin already given.

### Cited Findings
- **US 11,806,137 B2 "Real-time meal detection based on sensor glucose and estimated plasma insulin levels"** — assignee Medtronic MiniMed; inventors Anirban Roy, Benyamin Grosman, Neha J. Parikh; priority 2021-12-01; application 18/314,791 filed 2023-05-09; pre-grant pub US20230277097A1; granted 2023-11-07 **[patent]** — [Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11806137B2/en)
  - Inputs: SG every 5 min paired with plasma-insulin (Ip) estimates from a pharmacodynamic absorption model of subcutaneous insulin using "universal constants"; Ip normalised by 50% of a six-day median TDD.
  - Window: trailing 3 h (36 samples); 1-h and 4-h variants described; window "sized to include a fasting period and to exclude a period of postprandial glucose level increase"; missing values interpolated if <~25% (9/36) missing.
  - Classifier: logistic regression or LSTM (or ensemble) outputs probability that the window "corresponds to an ongoing glycemic response to a meal"; default threshold ~50%, 70–90% for higher confidence, ~40% for higher sensitivity; larger window allows lower threshold.
  - Onset: once flagged, look back up to three SG values to find where the rise began; figures show detection within ~3 SG samples of onset.
  - Reported accuracy: adding Ip raised accuracy from 91% to 96%, attributed to fewer false positives (e.g., sensor artefacts).
  - Action: notify user/caregiver, and/or "deliver several correction boluses instead of one meal bolus" by "temporarily lowering the correction target glucose and/or reducing the insulin sensitivity factor (ISF)," reverting when the response ends; a late announced meal bolus "is reduced by the total correction insulin already delivered for that meal."
  - Explicit dose caps/rate limits are not described in the retrieved text; hypo/hyper thresholds appear only in figures.
- Related Medtronic MiniMed patents (Justia listing): a patent filed 18 Feb 2021, issued 12 Dec 2023, inventors Grosman, Lintereur, Roy, Parikh, Wu, Weydt, Dunleavy, Dianaty (abstract adjacent in listing concerns using post-augmented-meal-bolus glucose to decide whether to reduce basal — attribution unconfirmed); US 12,311,145 (Grosman/Roy/Parikh) on detecting rescue-carbohydrate consumption and automatically limiting insulin delivery **[patent listing]** — [Justia: Benyamin Grosman](https://patents.justia.com/inventor/benyamin-grosman); [Justia: MiniMed assignee](https://patents.justia.com/assignee/minimed?page=8)
- Older Medtronic "Meal detection devices and methods" patents US 9,168,000 and US 10,791,988 exist (classification-only snippets; inventors not confirmed) **[patent]** — [US10791988](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10791988); [US9168000](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9168000)
- Medtronic feasibility of a cloud "meal prediction algorithm" (Cohort C, n=72, TIR 77%) and "Digital Twin" adaptation (Cohort A, n=54, TIR 70.5%), 2020–2022 — [NCT04203823](https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies/NCT04203823)
- Non-algorithmic route also explored: Klue smartwatch hand-gesture meal announcement (n=17, TIR 80.6%, ATTD 2023) — [Medtronic, 24 Feb 2023](https://news.medtronic.com/2023-02-24-MiniMed-TM-780G-system-demonstrates-superiority-over-multiple-daily-injections-with-CGM-for-type-1-diabetes-management-at-one-year-in-ADAPT-study)
- Safety logic in the shipping 780G that bounds unannounced-meal dosing: auto-corrections only when basal is saturated and SG >120; corrections suppressed at temp target 150 and in exercise mode; predicted-low subtraction from corrections; exits after 7 h at max basal — [AGDT fact sheet](https://www.diabetesnet.com/pdfs/MiniMed-780G-with-SmartGuard.pdf); [T1D Exchange](https://t1dexchange.org/medtronic-780g-hybrid-insulin-pump-features/)
- Management description of Vivera's meal handling: a forecast "works every five minutes" and adjusts dosing; "If carb data is entered, the algorithm uses it. If not, it detects the blood glucose rise and adjusts as best it can"; initialisation from TDD alone **[company/secondary]** — [BofA transcript](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/minimed-at-bank-of-america-global-healthcare-conference-growth-push-93CH-4912616); [T1D Family Centre](https://www.type1familycentre.org.au/post/what-should-count-as-a-fully-closed-loop-insulin-pump); [roadshow](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2062583/000162828026062885/minimed-425splitxoffroadsh.htm)

### Inferences
- The patent's "lower the correction target / shrink ISF while a meal response is detected, then revert" is a natural extension of the 780G's existing correction machinery (fixed 120 mg/dL correction target, learned ISF) and matches management's description of Vivera as the same controller with meal input made optional — i.e., meal detection changes the correction set-point/gain rather than issuing a single large meal bolus.
- Normalising plasma-insulin by TDD in the detector is consistent with "requires only total daily insulin dose to start": TDD is the single scaling parameter for both the detector and (per 780G lineage) the controller gains.
- Debiting a late user bolus by correction insulin already delivered is the mechanism that lets carbs be "optional at any time" without stacking.

### Gaps
- Claims text of US 11,806,137 was truncated in retrieval; exact claim scope not quoted.
- No peer-reviewed paper by Grosman/Roy/Parikh/Lintereur describing the Vivera/NMX-AID algorithm, its adaptation cadence ("hourly" is a blog claim), or its safety constraints was found. No published sensitivity/false-positive rates in free-living data beyond the patent's 91→96% accuracy figure.
