# Academic / Research-Stage State of the Art in AID Control Algorithms (as of Oct 2026)

Scope note: this survey is organized by function (detection, prediction, control, FCL clinical evidence, hypoglycemia prevention, insulin modeling, 2025–26 breakthroughs). All quantitative claims carry an inline source. Where a well-known primary paper could not be retrieved (several publisher pages returned HTTP 403), the number is given from the best secondary source and flagged, or moved to Gaps. Simulator availability is covered under Key Question 2 and 7.

## Key Question 1: Meal / disturbance detection from CGM (unannounced meals)

### Takeaway
The 2025/26 systematic review of meal-announcement-free algorithms finds median sensitivity ~88%, precision ~93%, and detection delays of ~25–40 min (range 10–60 min) across heuristic (rate-of-change/acceleration), model/estimator-based (Kalman filter + CUSUM, unscented KF, disturbance observers) and ML (LSTM, ensembles, RL bolus calculators) detectors; delay primarily costs time-above-range, false positives primarily cost safety, and in-silico studies systematically report more false positives than in-vivo ones. Adding heart rate (2026) cut false positives from ~3.3/day (ROC method) to ~0.5/day at a 28-min median detection time.

### Cited Findings
- Systematic review of meal-announcement-free algorithms (PMC13349122, 2025/26): median performance ~88% sensitivity, ~93% precision, detection time ~25–40 min; in-silico evaluations report more false positives than in-vivo; authors call for prospective validation with exercise and sensor artifacts — [Systematic review, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13349122/)
- Per-algorithm table from the same review (method, setting, sensitivity, precision/FP per day, delay, glycemic outcome of FAID vs comparator) — [Systematic review, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13349122/):
  - Atlas 2010 (heuristic, in vivo n=7): delay 23 min; TIR 73%, TAR 27%, TBR 0%.
  - Harvey 2014 (heuristic glucose-derivative detector, in silico n=10 + in vivo n=10): sensitivity 88%, FP 0.1/day, delay 39 min; TIR 74% (unannounced) vs 89% (announced); TBR 0% both.
  - Lee 2008 (heuristic, in silico): delay 30–40 min; mean CGM 137 vs 191 mg/dL.
  - Dovc 2020 (Cambridge FCL heuristic, in vivo n=20): delay 30.1–38.4 min; TIR 58.6% (standard aspart) vs 53.8% (faster aspart).
  - Samadi 2017 (fuzzy/heuristic, in silico): sensitivity 91.3%, precision 90.7%, FP 0.47/day; TIR 76.8% vs 85.4% (announced).
  - Pimentel 2020 (bang-bang on glucose-derivative thresholds, in silico): delay 30–45 min; TIR 90.7% vs 91.4%.
  - Xu 2021 (control/estimator-based, in silico): sensitivity 100%, precision 97.3%, FP 0.5/day, delay ≤60 min; TIR 96.6% vs 91.3%.
  - Mahmoudi 2018 (Kalman filter + CUSUM, in silico): sensitivity 80%, precision 100%, delay 40 min; TIR 71.6% vs 85.6%.
  - Mahmoudi 2019 (estimator-based, in silico): sensitivity 93%, precision 100%, delay 40 min; TIR 83% vs 91%.
  - Xie 2017 (estimator-based, in silico): sensitivity 76%, precision 84%, FP 0.44/day, delay 45 min; TIR 73% vs 85.5%; TBR 11% vs 5%.
  - Sala-Mira 2019 (disturbance-observer-based, in silico): sensitivity 90.5%, precision 97.4%, FP 0.1/day, delay 30 min; TIR 78.9% vs 86.9%.
  - Garcia-Tirado 2021 (UVA "RocketAP"/meal anticipation, in silico): delay 10–15 min (fastest reported); TIR 76.1%, TAR 23.8%, TBR 0%.
  - Carlos 2022 (control-based, in silico): sensitivity 66.6%, precision 81.5%, FP 0.6/day, delay 37.6 min; TIR 94.1% vs 90.9%.
  - Fushimi 2019 (in silico): delay 50–60 min; TIR 85% vs 83.3%.
  - Fathi 2019 (McGill, in vivo n=4): sensitivity 100%, 0 FP, delay 35 min; postprandial AUC ~39% lower than conventional.
  - Majdpour 2021 (McGill, in vivo n=9, insulin-only vs multihormonal insulin+pramlintide+glucagon): delay 30–40 min; insulin-only TIR 83.3%, TAR 12%, TBR 4.7%; multihormonal TIR 81%, TAR 18%, TBR 0.93%.
  - Daniels 2022 (LSTM-based, in silico): sensitivity 76%, precision 93%, F1 84, delay 38 min; TIR 77.8% vs 84.7%.
  - Mosquera-Lopez 2023 (ML, in vivo): sensitivity 83%, FP 1.0/day, delay 25.9 min; TIR +9.1 pts, TAR −10.8 pts, TBR stable vs hybrid CL.
  - Ibrahim 2024 (ensemble MLP/RF/LR, in silico UVA n=20 and Hovorka n=47): sensitivity 74.5–95%, precision 83.5–96%, FP 0.05–0.31/day, delay 21–31 min; TIR 68.2% vs 57.7% open loop (UVA).
  - Sayyar 2024 (deep-RL bolus calculator triggered by detector, in silico): sensitivity 64.3%, precision 89.9%, FP 0.29/day; personalized bolus computed by RL agent without CHO estimate; TIR 71.2% vs 76.2%.
- Review conclusions: detection delay typically 25–60 min; high sensitivity alone does not reliably improve TIR; longer delay mainly raises TAR rather than TBR; FP impact depends on insulin constraints/safety layers; benefit vs announced-meal hybrid CL is small, vs open loop is large; evidence mostly in silico — [Systematic review, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13349122/)
- Review's future directions: standard metrics (accuracy, latency, FP robustness), subject-wise validation, faster insulin/less CGM lag, use macronutrient estimates conservatively (not as triggers for large boluses), handle rescue carbs and exercise confounders, GAN-synthetic data — [Systematic review, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13349122/)
- Other in-vivo FCL detection results summarized in the same review: unscented-KF multivariable system (Turksoy/Cinar lineage, 7 young adults, 60 h): TIR +7.9%, TBR −2.3%, TAR −5.4% vs open loop; RocketAP vs hybrid CL over 6 h post-meal: TIR +30%, TAR −30%, no TBR increase; adolescent RCT n=11 FCL vs CSII: TIR 40.9% vs 20.5%; multiple-model probabilistic MPC 24-h inpatient: TIR +13.5%, TBR −2.27% vs open loop — [Systematic review, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13349122/)
- Ensemble ML meal detector (Computers in Biology and Medicine 2024): majority-voting ensemble reached 61% sensitivity, 72% precision, 0.17 FP/day on real OhioT1DM data vs 77% sensitivity and 0.05 FP/day in silico; filtering reduces FP but adds delay and lowers sensitivity — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0010482524002385)
- Rate-of-change heuristic detectors (Sensors 2018 "Unannounced Meals in the Artificial Pancreas"): across three tunings sensitivity ranged 99% to 47% as the FP constraint tightened; detection times 28–43 min — [Sensors 2018](https://doi.org/10.3390/s18030884)
- Simulation-and-explanation method (JAMIA 2019): combining a physiological simulation with CGM, insulin and heart-rate data detected meals ~10 min sooner than comparison on simulated data and produced fewer FP on real-world data — [JAMIA](https://academic.oup.com/jamia/article/26/12/1592/5575393)
- Online meal detection from CGM dynamics (arXiv July 2025): logistic-regression detector trained on UVA/Padova data outperformed a clinically validated benchmark on true-positive rate and detection delay; residual errors attributed largely to imprecise meal / hypo-treatment logging — [arXiv 2507.00080](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.00080v1)
- CGM + heart-rate meal detection (Frontiers in Digital Health 2026): median detection time 28 min, ~0.5 FP/day vs ~3.3 FP/day for a rate-of-change method, at the cost of lower recall — [Frontiers Digital Health 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/digital-health/articles/10.3389/fdgth.2026.1758474/full)
- Medtronic acquired Klue (smartwatch hand-gesture eating detection) and evaluated 780G + Klue "to clear the path to a true hands-free closed loop system"; a separate 780G study without meal announcement (≤80 g CHO meals) is reported in secondary sources — [MedTech Dive](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/medtronic-diabetes-acquires-klue-for-personalized-closed-loop/569267/); [Fierce Biotech](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/medtech/medtronic-pads-case-minimed-closed-loop-system-1-year-data-study-automated-meal-detection)
- UVA DCLP6 (NCT04877730) tested "meal anticipation" on the RocketAP algorithm; primary completion March 2022 — [ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04877730)

### Inferences
- A practical design envelope for a new detector: aim for ≤25–30 min detection delay at ≤0.5 FP/day; anything slower mostly buys TAR reduction and anything noisier mostly buys hypoglycemia risk, so the dosing response to a detection should be sized by the controller's safety layer (IOB cap, risk-weighted scaling) rather than a fixed meal bolus.
- Estimator-based detectors (KF/UKF with rate-of-appearance state, CUSUM on innovations, disturbance observers) consistently achieve precision ≥97% in silico at 30–40 min delay; heuristic ROC/acceleration thresholds get similar delay but worse FP unless filtered; ML detectors offer the shortest in-vivo delays (~26 min, Mosquera-Lopez) but at 1 FP/day.
- The review's in-silico-vs-in-vivo FP discrepancy suggests simulator meal/noise realism is the limiting factor for tuning, so any new detector should be tuned on real AID data (e.g., Nightscout/OpenAPS exports) rather than only UVA/Padova.
- Multimodal signals (heart rate, gesture) buy roughly a 6x reduction in FP at the same delay — a strong argument for optional wearable inputs in an FCL design.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve the second half of the systematic review (text truncated at 100k chars), so some per-study details (exact bolus logic on detection) are unknown; most studies did not specify the bolus triggered.
- The original Dassau 2008 meal detection (voting among Kalman-filtered derivative estimators), Turksoy's UKF multivariable AP equations, Samadi's fuzzy logic rule base, Weinzimer's early FCL, and Ramkissoon's work were not retrieved as primary sources; only their performance as summarized in the review is cited.
- No source gave a head-to-head false-positive rate for the Dovc/Hovorka Cambridge heuristic in free-living conditions.

## Key Question 2: Glucose prediction models used in or around control

### Takeaway
Deep models (LSTM/Transformer hybrids) report 30-min RMSE of ~13–20 mg/dL on OhioT1DM under favorable protocols but ~25–31 mg/dL under leave-one-patient-out, while the 2026 Nature CGM foundation model (GluFormer) targets long-horizon risk stratification rather than 30-min control-loop forecasting; inside actual controllers, the dominant predictors remain low-order LTI/ARX models (zone MPC, 45-min horizon) and simple linear regression (Basal-IQ, 30-min horizon).

### Cited Findings
- GluFormer (Nature, published online Jan 2026, DOI 10.1038/s41586-025-09925-9): generative transformer trained with self-supervised autoregressive token prediction on >10 million glucose measurements from 10,812 adults mainly without diabetes; representations transferred to 19 external cohorts (n=6,044; 5 countries, 8 CGM devices) spanning prediabetes, T1D, T2D, GDM, obesity; in 580 adults with median 11-year follow-up the top quartile captured 66% of new-onset diabetes diagnoses vs 7% in the bottom quartile; code on GitHub — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09925-9); [GitHub Guylu/GluFormer](https://github.com/Guylu/GluFormer); [arXiv 2408.11876 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.11876)
- Time-series foundation models vs dietary context for CGM forecasting (arXiv Sept 2026) and personalized forecasting with foundational AI in T1D/T2D (arXiv Jan 2026) exist as preprints — [arXiv 2609.11872](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.11872); [arXiv 2601.00613](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.00613)
- OhioT1DM 30-min RMSE benchmarks (mg/dL): stacked LSTM 16.06 ± 2.74 (2023); bidirectional LSTM 20.20 at 30 min and 34.19 at 60 min (2020); LSTM-based 18.87; temporal fusion transformer 19.10 (OhioT1DM) and 12.70 (ShanghaiT1DM); DA-CMTL multi-task model 13.38 (OhioT1DM) and 10.58 (ShanghaiT1DM) — [PMC11970073](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11970073); [npj Digital Medicine 2025 multi-task sim-to-real](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-025-01994-4)
- Leave-one-patient-out protocol gives much worse numbers: BiT-MAML (Bi-LSTM + Transformer + meta-learning) 24.89 mg/dL at 30 min vs re-implemented LSTM 30.86 and Edge-LSTM 29.00 — [Scientific Reports 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-13491-5)
- Incrementally retrained personalized LSTM from limited CGM data (2025) — [PMC11999170](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11999170)
- Zone-MPC internal predictor (Gondhalekar/Dassau/Doyle, Automatica 2018): discrete-time LTI, T=5 min, x_{i+1}=Ax_i+Bu_i, A=[[p1+2p2, −2p1p2−p2², p1p2²],[1,0,0],[0,1,0]], C_y=[0 0 1], C_v=[0.1 0 −0.1], K=90(p1−1)(p2−1)², p1=0.98, p2=0.965 (subject-independent poles), personalized only by TDI; prediction horizon N_y=9 steps (45 min) — [Automatica 2018, PMC6051553](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6051553/)
- Basal-IQ (Tandem) uses a simple linear regression to predict glucose 30 min ahead and suspends if predicted <80 mg/dL within 30 min or CGM <70 mg/dL — [JMIR Diabetes 2021](https://diabetes.jmir.org/2021/2/e26909/)
- A PPO deep-RL controller used the last hour of CGM as its full state (no explicit predictor) — [PLOS ONE 2025](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0317662)
- Simulators available for validating prediction/control: simglucose (MIT-licensed Python implementation of the 2008 UVA/Padova model, built for RL; a 2026 extension adds cortisol/growth-hormone counter-regulation and physical activity) — [PyPI simglucose](https://pypi.org/project/simglucose/0.1.5/); [Extended Simglucose 2026](https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2026/151886/151886.pdf); ReplayBG (digital-twin MATLAB toolbox identifying a personalized model from retrospective T1D data; validated on 100 UVA/Padova virtual subjects; Python port PyReplayBG) — [ReplayBG, IEEE TBME 2023](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/371911244_ReplayBG_a_digital_twin-based_methodology_to_identify_a_personalized_model_from_type_1_diabetes_data_and_simulate_glucose_concentrations_to_assess_alternative_therapies); UVA/Padova simulator (FDA-accepted 2008 as animal-trial substitute; current version holds >6,000 digital twins of T1D and T2D) — [UVA CDT](https://med.virginia.edu/diabetes-technology/2025/06/10/digital-twin-technology/); [Cobelli & Kovatchev JDST 2023](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/19322968231195081); T1DMSIM (seed-driven open-source simulator benchmarked against OhioT1DM, ShanghaiT1DM, AZT1D and simglucose) — [GitHub T1DMSIM](https://github.com/0xdeadf1sh/T1DMSIM); TRSET (simulation of Tidepool Loop for hazard estimation) and a curated list — [awesome-diabetes-software](https://github.com/gcappon/awesome-diabetes-software); Sirael virtual metabolic machine — [Springer 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44174-024-00199-9); open-source closed-loop testbed — [arXiv 2208.06479](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.06479)

### Inferences
- Reported RMSE values are not comparable across papers (differences in splits, Kalman smoothing, inputs); a defensible planning figure for a well-tuned personalized deep model is ~15–20 mg/dL at 30 min and ~30–35 mg/dL at 60 min, and ~25–30 mg/dL at 30 min for population models on unseen patients.
- No retrieved paper demonstrated a Transformer/LSTM forecaster embedded in a clinically tested insulin controller; the clinically deployed predictors are all low-order linear models. Foundation models (GluFormer) are, as of Oct 2026, representation/risk tools, not control-loop forecasters.
- For a new algorithm the pragmatic choice is a low-order physiological/ARX predictor for the control cost plus a separate data-driven detector/forecaster for meal and hypo events (where the 30-min horizon error of ~15–25 mg/dL is tolerable).

### Gaps
- No source quantified 30/60-min RMSE for UVA/Padova- or Hovorka-model-based predictors inside MPC controllers (they are evaluated by closed-loop outcomes, not forecast error).
- "Dexcom's AI" forecasting and Apple/Google health forecasting work: no primary source retrieved (see KQ7).
- DMMS.R (Epsilon Group's commercial UVA/Padova derivative) and the Tidepool simulator: no primary documentation retrieved.

## Key Question 3: Control strategies (PID, MPC variants, fuzzy, RL, run-to-run / ILC)

### Takeaway
The best-documented research controller is Doyle/Dassau zone MPC with asymmetric costs, velocity weighting and a velocity penalty (the lineage of Omnipod 5's MPC), which in silico cut time <70 mg/dL from 2.02% to 0.51% at equal TIR on unannounced 90 g meals; deep RL (PPO/SAC) reaches 87% median TIR fully closed loop in simglucose but remains in-silico-only; run-to-run/ILC adaptation of basal and ICR is well developed in silico with only pilot-level clinical use.

### Cited Findings
- Zone MPC cost (Gondhalekar, Dassau, Doyle, Automatica 2018): J = Σ_{k=1}^{Ny} [ ž_k² + Q(v_k)·ẑ_k² + D̂·v̂_k² ] + Σ_{k=0}^{Nu−1} [ R̂·û_k² + Ř·ǔ_k² ], with ẑ=max(z,0), ž=min(z,0) (asymmetric zone excursions), û/ǔ positive/negative input deviations from basal, v̂=max(v,0); zone Z(y,i) is the signed distance to the diurnal target zone [80,140] mg/dL 06:00–22:00 and [90,140] 24:00–04:00 with 2-h transitions; horizons N_y=9 (45 min), N_u=5 (25 min); weights R̂=6500, Ř=100, D=1000, ε=1e-6; constraints: input interval containing 0, basal assumed beyond control horizon, diurnal insulin caps and an IOB constraint — [Automatica 2018, PMC6051553](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6051553/)
- Velocity weighting: Q(v)=1 for v ≥ 0; Q(v)=ε for v ≤ −1 mg/dL/min; between, Q(v)=½[cos(vπ)(1−ε)+(1+ε)], so hyperglycemia correction is fully penalized while BG is rising/flat and fades as BG falls. Velocity penalty: D̂=D=1000 when current BG ∈ [140,180] mg/dL else 0, giving an early assertive response at the start of rises — [Automatica 2018, PMC6051553](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6051553/)
- Zone-MPC in-silico results (UVA/Padova, 111 subjects, 1221 simulations, unannounced 90 g CHO meals), proposed vs previous MPC: TIR 61.86% vs 61.00%; time <70 0.51% vs 2.02%; <50 0.12% vs 0.99%; pump suspensions ≥60 min 318 vs 761; mean LBGI 0.18 vs 0.77; insulin 42.77 vs 43.63 U; announced meals <70: 0.07% vs 0.42%. Tested in four FDA-approved campaigns (6 at home pilot; 10 adolescents 3 days; 19 adults 14 days; 29 adults 3 months). Runs on a Nexus 5 phone, ~1.2 s per call, QP solved typically in one iteration (cap 10) — [Automatica 2018, PMC6051553](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6051553/)
- Lineage: zone cost first by Grosman 2010; periodic zone + asymmetric input costs Gondhalekar/Dassau/Doyle 2016 (Automatica); velocity weighting 2015; velocity penalty 2014; Shi/Dassau/Doyle 2018 adapt penalties by glucose and velocity; VWMPC reduced hypoglycemia while VPMPC was more effective at correcting hyperglycemia — [Periodic zone-MPC, Automatica 2016](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0005109816301388); [Adaptive zone MPC 2018](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/327144019_Adaptive_Zone_Model_Predictive_Control_of_Artificial_Pancreas_Based_on_Glucose-_and_Velocity-Dependent_Control_Penalties); [WO2017004278A1 patent](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2017004278A1/en)
- Omnipod 5: MPC "based on control strategies published by Doyle, Dassau, Gondhalekar and Lee"; microboluses every 5 min, 60-min prediction horizon, control-to-target with user-set target, asymmetric cost weighting hypoglycemia more heavily, "medically inspired personalization scheme" for insulin-sensitivity variation; algorithm resides on each Pod — [Omnipod 5 review, PMC8097502](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8097502/); [Omnipod FAQ](https://www.omnipod.com/current-podders/resources/omnipod-5/faqs/smartadjust-technology); 110 mg/dL target safe in adults/adolescents — [ADA 978-P](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/342187970_978-P_Omnipod_Personalized_MPC_Algorithm_at_Target_Glucose_of_110mgdl_Is_Safe_in_Adults_and_Adolescents_without_Increasing_Risk_of_Hypoglycemia)
- Control-IQ lineage: Kovatchev holds the patents behind Control-IQ; "risk space" control is Patek, Breton, Vereshchetin, Jiang, Kovatchev, IFAC 2014 "Model-Based Control of Type 1 Diabetes in 'Risk Space'"; symmetrization of the BG scale is Kovatchev et al., Diabetes Care 1997;20:1655–1658 — [UVA faculty page](https://med.virginia.edu/diabetes-technology/leadership/faculty/boris-kovatchev-phd/); [USPTO 10638981 (actionable glycemic risk)](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10638981); [USPTO 10420489 (modular architecture)](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10420489)
- UVA next-generation: "Neural-net Artificial Pancreas (NAP)" trial protocol NCT05876273 and Adaptive Biobehavioral Control (ABC) NCT04784637 exist — [NAP protocol](https://cdn.clinicaltrials.gov/large-docs/73/NCT05876273/Prot_SAP_000.pdf); [ABC protocol](https://cdn.clinicaltrials.gov/large-docs/37/NCT04784637/Prot_SAP_000.pdf)
- Tandem is advancing UVA's "ADANET" algorithm toward a fully closed loop requiring no meal announcements (Q2 2026 earnings call, Oct 2026) — [Benzinga transcript](https://www.benzinga.com/news/26/10/62293535/tandem-diabetes-care-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript)
- Deep RL FCL (Zhao et al., PLOS ONE Jan 2025): PPO with ten modifications (Beta policy on [0,1], advantage normalization, treatment-failure flag, state normalization, entropy, LR decay 2e-5→0); state = last 60 min of CGM, no meal info; action = continuous infusion rate (0–30 U range); reward from Clarke/risk index in (0,1), halved outside 70–180, 0 below 50 mg/dL, target 112.5 mg/dL; safety module scales dose by Insulin_coef (0.8 after 20:00, 0.6 before 03:00, 0 when BG <80, plus deviation/trend terms); simglucose v0.2.1, 30 virtual patients (10 adults/adolescents/children), 1-day episodes, meals ±20% size ±30 min; results median TIR 87.45% (IQR 79.9–91.45) vs PID-with-announcements 76.53% vs SAC (Fox) 72.68%; TBR 0.75% vs 2.87% vs 0.73%; TAR 11.75% vs 20.06% vs 26.17%; max dose 0.52 U; >4,000 training episodes per patient; ~30% of patients TAR ≥20% — [PLOS ONE 2025](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0317662)
- Bioinspired RL FCL (Lee et al., IEEE JBHI 2021; UVA/Padova with unannounced meals, insulin-sensitivity variation, dawn phenomenon): mean glucose 124.72 mg/dL, 89.56% TIR — [IEEE 9115809](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9115809/)
- Offline RL for safer BG control (Emerson et al., arXiv 2022 / J Biomed Inform 2023): BCQ, CQL, TD3-BC on 30 virtual patients in hybrid closed loop; tested robustness to missing CGM and mealtime overdosing — [arXiv 2204.03376](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.03376); [J Biomed Inform](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1532046423000977); end-to-end offline RL for glycemia control — [arXiv 2310.10312](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.10312)
- Safety-improved RL AP (preprint Oct–Nov 2025): falls back to most recent safe policy when an update degrades safety; 20 simglucose subjects; lower failure rates vs conventional PPO especially in high-risk scenarios — [ResearchGate 397171475](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/397171475_Improving_Safety_in_Reinforcement_Learning-based_Artificial_Pancreas_Systems)
- Personalized insulin adjustment with RL for intensive insulin therapy, in-silico (arXiv May 2025) — [arXiv 2505.14477](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.14477); DRL event-triggered control for networked AP (arXiv Apr 2026) — [arXiv 2604.26126](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.26126); DQN dosing recommendations on real OhioT1DM data (JMIR Diabetes 2026) — [JMIR Diabetes 2026](https://diabetes.jmir.org/2026/1/e79195/PDF)
- Run-to-run / ILC: IEEE TBME 2020 pairs ILC for daily basal with R2R for mealtime ICR, weekly updates from sparse SMBG (in silico) — [IEEE TBME 2020](https://doi.org/10.1109/tbme.2020.3005622); [PubMed 32746043](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32746043); multivariate learning framework for long-term AP adaptation — [PMC6336673](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6336673/); ILC described as a two-timescale enhancement of R2R; iterative-learning MPC adapting the reference trajectory tested in a pilot study; Palerm 2008 and Magni 2009 R2R basal/MPC tuning, Toffanin 2017 R2R basal — [Data-enabled learning review, Annual Reviews in Control 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1367578823000615)
- Personalized basal tuner detecting basal-rate inaccuracies without meal data (retrospective validation, 2025) — [PMC12661609](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12661609/)
- Data-driven personalization of AID (arXiv June 2026, in silico 100-adult UVA/Padova cohort): TIR gains of 2, 3 and 4 percentage points after 4, 8 and 17 weeks of tuning — [arXiv 2606.17004](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.17004)
- Gaussian-process MPC for AID (arXiv 2021) — [arXiv 2101.11887](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.11887); simultaneous MPC + moving-horizon estimation (Copp 2018) — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/oca.2388)
- Open-source algorithms (OpenAPS/Loop-type) compared head-to-head in a full-closed-loop pig model — [PMC8087942](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8087942/)

### Inferences
- The combination that produced the largest safety gain at constant TIR in the Doyle group's work is (a) an asymmetric zone cost, (b) velocity weighting that stops stacking corrections once BG is already falling ≥1 mg/dL/min, and (c) an explicit velocity penalty only in the 140–180 band to act early on rises. All three are directly transferable to a Trio-style controller that currently uses a single predicted-BG target.
- RL results (87–90% TIR fully closed loop) are consistently obtained on simglucose/UVA/Padova with simplified meal patterns and should be read as upper bounds; no RL controller has a clinical trial as of Oct 2026 in the retrieved sources. The explicit safety layer in Zhao et al. (time-of-day coefficient, zero dose below 80 mg/dL) is essentially a hand-written rule system on top of the policy.
- R2R/ILC for slow parameters (basal profile, ICR, ISF) is low-risk and clinically plausible; the 2–4 pp TIR gain over 4–17 weeks in silico sets expectations.

### Gaps
- Medtronic PID + insulin feedback (IFB) equations, Hovorka's Cambridge MPC formulation, DreaMed MD-Logic fuzzy rules, Omnipod 5's exact cost function, Control-IQ's exact risk-space mapping and the Tejedor RL survey were not retrieved as primary sources in this run; treat these as known-but-uncited.
- No large RCT of ILC-based basal adaptation inside AID found.

## Key Question 4: Fully closed-loop clinical evidence

### Takeaway
Insulin-only FCL achieves roughly 50% TIR in adults with T1D and HbA1c ≥8% (CamAPS HX + Lyumjev, secondary-source figure), 45% vs 32% in adolescents, 61% vs 56% in a 72-h Tandem feasibility study, and 66% vs 32% in T2D; bihormonal (insulin + glucagon) Inreda FCL reaches 80% TIR at 1 year in real-world use (86.6% in a 2-week RCT); adding pramlintide to a lispro FCL did not improve TIR overall (71% vs 72%) but helped those starting below 70% TIR.

### Cited Findings
- CLEAR (Cambridge, Boughton et al., Diabetes Care 2023;46:1916–1922): single-center RCT crossover, 26 adults with T1D on pumps, HbA1c ≥8.0% (baseline 9.2 ± 1.1%), 8 weeks CamAPS HX FCL + Lyumjev vs 8 weeks usual pump + CGM — [Diabetes Care 2023](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article-abstract/46/11/1916/153534/Fully-Closed-Loop-Glucose-Control-Compared-With); [Lived experience paper, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10979660/). Secondary report: TIR went "from 36% to 50%" without announcing meals — [diaTribe](https://diatribe.org/diabetes-technology/fully-closed-loop-automated-insulin-delivery-takes-next-step) (primary figures not retrieved; see Gaps)
- CLEAR Phase 2 (adolescents with sub-optimal HbA1c, CamAPS HX FCL with Fiasp vs non-automated pump + sensor): TIR 45% vs 32%, mean difference 12.9 pp — [Diabetes Research and Clinical Practice 2025](https://www.diabetesresearchclinicalpractice.com/article/S0168-8227(25)01093-9/fulltext)
- Cambridge FCL in T2D (Daly et al., Nature Medicine 2023; 26 adults, CamAPS HX with Fiasp, crossover): TIR 66.3 ± 14.9% vs 32.3 ± 24.7% control (difference 35.3 pp) — [Nature Medicine](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-022-02144-z); in T2D on dialysis (Nature Medicine 2021): >3.5 additional hours/day in range — [Nature Medicine 2021](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-021-01453-z)
- Hypo-/hyperglycemia patterns under FCL in T1D vs T2D adults (Cambridge, JDST 2025) — [JDST 2025](https://doi.org/10.1177/19322968241242803)

- Inpatient Lyumjev vs lispro FCL (n=17, 8-h sessions, one unannounced meal): TIR 49.3% vs 39.9%, p=0.072 (blog-sourced; low reliability) — [Diabettech](https://www.diabettech.com/losing-lyumjev-how-the-system-failed-patients/)

- Beta Bionics iLet insulin-only pivotal (NEJM 2022, 13 weeks, n=326, 2:1): HbA1c 7.9%→7.3%; TIR 65 ± 9% vs 54 ± 17% usual care (+11 pp ≈ 2.6 h/day); FDA cleared 2023; iLet uses only body weight for initialization and qualitative meal announcements — [NEJM 2022](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2205225); [ClinicalTrials.gov results NCT04200313](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04200313); primary-care/telehealth RCT replicated results — [Clinical Diabetes 2024](https://diabetesjournals.org/clinical/article/43/3/388/157928/Assessing-the-iLet-Bionic-Pancreas-Deployed-in)

June 2026) — [PLOS ONE protocol](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0335060); 2026 narrative review: semaglutide/tirzepatide + AID associated with weight loss, lower TDD, higher TIR without more hypoglycemia/DKA — [Narrative review 2026](https://doi.org/10.1177/15209156261486127)
- Medtronic: CE mark for 780G with Abbott Instinct sensor; 780G "offers meal detection technology" per ATTD 2026 reporting — [Drug Delivery Business ATTD 2026](https://www.drugdeliverybusiness.com/biggest-diabetes-tech-news-attd-2026/)

### Inferences
- Insulin-only FCL with current ultra-rapid analogs plateaus around 50–65% TIR in T1D with high baseline HbA1c, and ~68% in T2D; the ~80–87% TIR numbers in FCL come only with a second hormone (glucagon) or in silico. Post-meal peaks are the binding constraint (TAR, not TBR, is what grows with detection delay per KQ1).
- Pramlintide's benefit in FCL appears restricted to people with poorer baseline control, and GI tolerability is a real cost; GLP-1 adjuncts reduce TDD and increase TIR but are drug therapy, not algorithm design.
- The Inreda 1-year result (80.3% TIR with SD 5.4) shows that glucagon availability dramatically reduces inter-subject variance, implying that insulin-only algorithms must instead rely on conservative aggressiveness scaling to achieve comparable safety.

### Gaps
- Primary CLEAR adult TIR/TBR/HbA1c values not retrieved (Diabetes Care page 403, Europe PMC query did not return it); the 36%→50% figure is from diaTribe.
- Postprandial peak glucose values for FCL studies were not available from any retrieved abstract.
- Medtronic FCL trial (adaptive meal detection, 2025–26) results: not found in the retrieved sources.

## Key Question 5: Hypoglycemia prevention (PLGS, exercise awareness, dual hormone, risk-based aggressiveness)

### Takeaway
Clinically proven building blocks are: a 30-min linear-regression predictive suspend (Basal-IQ: <70 median TBR 2.6% vs 3.2% SAP, no mean-glucose penalty), heart-rate-informed control-to-range (reduced exercise BG decline, p=0.022), asymmetric/velocity-weighted MPC costs (time <70 cut 4x in silico), and glucagon as a second hormone (TBR 0.4% vs 2%); the Kovatchev risk function remains the standard aggressiveness-scaling primitive, and ML hypo-alert models reach >97% sensitivity/specificity at 30- and 60-min horizons.

### Cited Findings
- Basal-IQ / PLGS rule: simple linear regression predicts 30 min ahead; suspend if predicted <80 mg/dL within 30 min or current CGM <70 mg/dL (Dexcom G6 version: predicted <80 or currently <70 and falling) — [JMIR Diabetes 2021](https://diabetes.jmir.org/2021/2/e26909/); [diaTribe FDA approval](https://diatribe.org/diabetes-technology/fda-approves-tandems-basal-iq-predictive-low-glucose-suspend-system)
- PROLOG RCT (6-week crossover, n=103, T1D, ages 6+): median time <70 mg/dL 3.6% baseline → 2.6% PLGS vs 3.2% SAP; mean glucose unchanged 159 vs 159 mg/dL (p=0.40) — [Diabetes Care 2018](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/41/10/2155/36689/Predictive-Low-Glucose-Suspend-Reduces)
- Improved low-glucose predictive alerts based on sustained hypoglycemia (JMIR 2021): ML model predicts sustained events with >97% sensitivity and specificity at 30- and 60-min horizons, using data partly from Basal-IQ users — [JMIR Diabetes 2021](https://diabetes.jmir.org/2021/2/e26909/)
- PLGS necessitates less carbohydrate to rescue hypoglycemia (revisit 15 g rule) — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/349016550_Predictive_Low-Glucose_Suspend_Necessitates_Less_Carbohydrate_Supplementation_to_Rescue_Hypoglycemia_Need_to_Revisit_Current_Hypoglycemia_Treatment_Guidelines)
- Breton 2014 (heart rate into control-to-range AP): significantly reduced BG decline during exercise (p=0.022); hypo events during exercise 2→0 (p=0.16, NS); TIR 81% vs 75% (NS) — [PubMed 24702135](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24702135/); adolescent HR trial NCT01945060 — [ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01945060)
- Jacobs 2015 (regression on HR + accelerometer detects/grades mild aerobic exercise with high sensitivity/specificity; 21-adult crossover, 22-h sessions): time in euglycemia 67% with exercise detection vs 72% without vs 68% SAP (detection arm manually triggered; no overall benefit) — [JDST 2015 PDF](https://europepmc.org/api/getPdf?pmcid=PMC4667295); [JMIR narrative review](https://diabetes.jmir.org/2022/1/e28861/)
- Jacobs dual-hormone with automated exercise detection (preliminary, 17 visits): activity trackers detected exercise in 85% of participants; time in hypoglycemia 1% dual-hormone vs 43.4% single-hormone (subgroup) — [Healio 2017](https://www.healio.com/news/endocrinology/20170614/automated-exercise-detection-with-dualhormone-artificial-pancreas-system-may-reduce-hypoglycemia)
- Resalat/Jacobs 2019: adaptive AP with model identification, adaptive postprandial insulin, and HR + accelerometry as control inputs — [JDST 2019](https://doi.org/10.1177/1932296819881467)
- Ensemble learning exercise detection in T1D (arXiv 2023) — [arXiv 2305.10353](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.10353); UVA patents on predictive hypoglycemia detection for mild-to-moderate exercise — [USPTO 12336847](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12336847)
- DeBoer adolescent trial: reduced hypoglycemic events using HR in an activity-augmented control architecture — [JMIR narrative review](https://diabetes.jmir.org/2022/1/e28861/)
- Zone-MPC safety layers: diurnal insulin caps, IOB constraint, velocity weighting; in silico time <70 0.51% vs 2.02%, <50 0.12% vs 0.99%, LBGI 0.18 vs 0.77, pump suspensions ≥120 min 37 vs 282 — [Automatica 2018, PMC6051553](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6051553/)
- Risk-space control: Kovatchev symmetrization (Diabetes Care 1997) and Patek et al. IFAC 2014 "risk space" MPC underlie Control-IQ; USPTO 10638981 "assessing actionable glycemic risk" — [UVA](https://med.virginia.edu/diabetes-technology/leadership/faculty/boris-kovatchev-phd/); [USPTO 10638981](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10638981)
- RL reward based on Clarke risk index with hard-zero reward below 50 mg/dL and a dose coefficient forced to 0 below 80 mg/dL — [PLOS ONE 2025](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0317662)
- Glucagon/dual-hormone: Inreda TBR 0.4% vs 2% (RCT) and 1.36% median at 1 year — [Diabetes Care 2021](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/44/3/836/138610/Fully-Closed-Loop-Glucose-Control-With-a); [Lancet DH 2024 via Europe PMC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=bihormonal%20fully%20closed-loop%20real-world%20multicentre%20Netherlands%20Lancet%20Digital%20Health%202024&resultType=core&format=json&pageSize=3); Xeris glucagon 3-way crossover closed-loop protocol — [NCT03424044](https://cdn.clinicaltrials.gov/large-docs/44/NCT03424044/Prot_SAP_000.pdf)

### Inferences
- The simplest high-value hypo layer for an insulin-only system is a 30-min linear extrapolation suspend with an 80 mg/dL predicted threshold; adding a sustained-hypo ML classifier can raise alert specificity without changing the suspend rule.
- Exercise detection from wearables reliably detects exercise but only translates into glycemic benefit when it is wired automatically into the controller (manual triggering showed none).
- Velocity-weighted costs are a controller-level analogue of "don't correct a falling glucose" and gave a ~4x reduction in time <70 in silico without TIR loss — likely the single most transferable idea for a predicted-BG-targeting controller.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed accuracy (RMSE/sensitivity) of Basal-IQ's own 30-min regression was found.
- No primary text of the Kovatchev risk-function equations or Control-IQ's aggressiveness mapping was retrieved.
- Dexcom + Garmin exercise integration: no primary source found.

## Key Question 6: Insulin modeling (action curves, PK of ultra-rapid analogs, IOB, circadian ISF, digital twins, TDD scaling)

### Takeaway
Ultra-rapid analogs shorten action (Lyumjev onset 10 min faster and duration 44 min shorter than Humalog; Fiasp peak 25.7 min earlier and duration 35.4 min shorter than aspart; Fiasp onset 16–20 min, peak ~63 min, duration 5–7 h), the TDD→ISF relationship was fit as ISF ≈ 1736/TDD in the morning in a pediatric cohort (close to the 1800 rule), and UVA's digital-twin "Adaptive Biobehavioral Control" RCT (n=72, 6 months) raised TIR 72%→77% and HbA1c 6.8%→6.6% by re-optimizing AID parameters bi-weekly.

### Cited Findings
- Lyumjev (lispro-aabc, excipients treprostinil + sodium citrate): onset of action 10 min faster and duration 44 min shorter than Humalog in euglycemic clamps — [Control-IQ + Lyumjev protocol NCT05403502](https://cdn.clinicaltrials.gov/large-docs/02/NCT05403502/Prot_000.pdf); Control-IQ Lyumjev safety study — [ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05403502); 780G Lyumjev mealtime vs post-meal dosing — [NCT06600776](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06600776); AP@Home04 Lyumjev vs Humalog closed loop (completed, results not retrieved) — [NCT05257460](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05257460)
- Fiasp (niacinamide + L-arginine): in pump use, time to half-maximal activity −11.8 min, time to peak −25.7 min, duration −35.4 min vs aspart; review table: onset 16–20 min, peak 63 min, duration 5–7 h — [Clinical Diabetology 2018](https://journals.viamedica.pl/clinical_diabetology/article/download/DK.2018.0031/47303); [Ultra-rapid insulins review, PMC8603316](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8603316/); Fiasp in 670G HCL protocol — [NCT03554486](https://cdn.clinicaltrials.gov/large-docs/86/NCT03554486/Prot_SAP_000.pdf)
- Clinical effect of faster insulin in FCL was not automatically positive: Cambridge FCL TIR 58.6% standard aspart vs 53.8% faster aspart (Dovc 2020) — [Systematic review table](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13349122/)
- Dynamic IOB incorporating circadian insulin-sensitivity variation: defines a diurnal IS curve for T1D adults from clinical dawn-phenomenon studies, noting prior approximations lacked physiological grounding — [Dynamic IOB paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/255176157_Dynamic_Insulin_on_Board_Incorporation_of_Circadian_Insulin_Sensitivity_Variation); dawn phenomenon common in both T1D and T2D — [NEJM 1984](https://doi.org/10.1056/nejm198403223101203); diurnal IS cycling in T2D deviates from physiology (nadir a.m.) — [Diabetes 2023](https://diabetesjournals.org/diabetes/article/72/10/1364/153373/Diurnal-Cycling-of-Insulin-Sensitivity-in-Type-2); probabilistic CGM estimate of dawn phenomenon prevalence 49% (95% CI 37–63%) in T2D — [PMC10844336](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10844336/); in-silico RL studies model IS dropping to 50% nominal 04:00–08:00 with 30-min ramps (assumption) — [arXiv 1901.01816](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.01816)
- TDD→ISF: pediatric regression of time-of-day ISF on 1/TDD (zero intercept) gave morning ISF = 1736/TDD (95% CI 1699–1774) — [Frontiers in Pediatrics 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/pediatrics/articles/10.3389/fped.2022.854972/full); rules of thumb: ICR = 500/TDD (450 for regular insulin), ISF = 1800/TDD (1500 more aggressive), described as conservative and often underestimating needs — [TCOYD 2026](https://tcoyd.org/2026/03/hybrid-closed-loop-insulin-pump-settings/); [USPTO 8998878](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8998878); one patent notes the accuracy of rule-derived IS is low — [USPTO 9244077](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9244077)
- Zone MPC personalizes its LTI model solely through TDI (gain K scaled by u_TDI) — [Automatica 2018, PMC6051553](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6051553/)
- UVA digital twin RCT (npj Digital Medicine May 2025): 72 T1D participants, 6 months; each person mapped to a digital twin; AID control parameters optimized bi-weekly; users could run what-if simulations; TIR 72%→77%, HbA1c 6.8%→6.6%; larger gains for baseline HbA1c >7% (secondary source) — [npj Digital Medicine 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-025-01679-y); [UVA CDT](https://med.virginia.edu/diabetes-technology/2025/06/10/digital-twin-technology/); [Medical Xpress](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2025-06-artificial-pancreas-digital-twin-tech.html)
- Real-time digital twin for T1D using simulation-based inference (arXiv July 2025) — [arXiv 2507.01740](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.01740); ReplayBG identifies personalized models from retrospective data — [ReplayBG](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/371911244_ReplayBG_a_digital_twin-based_methodology_to_identify_a_personalized_model_from_type_1_diabetes_data_and_simulate_glucose_concentrations_to_assess_alternative_therapies)
- Medtronic patents on selectable insulin absorption models and correction factors in pumps — [USPTO 8734428](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8734428)

### Inferences
- The empirical 1736/TDD fit (pediatric) supports the 1800 rule as a central estimate but with time-of-day variation; a new algorithm can use TDD-scaled ISF as a prior and let a slow R2R/ILC loop or digital-twin fit adjust it per time block.
- Because Fiasp did not outperform standard aspart in Cambridge FCL while Lyumjev-based FCL was used in CLEAR, insulin-model timing parameters (peak/DIA) should be insulin-specific and configurable (e.g., ~45–55 min peak and ~5 h duration for Fiasp per the PK data) rather than one fixed curve.
- Digital-twin parameter re-optimization every ~2 weeks is clinically validated (+5 pp TIR) and is the most evidence-backed form of "personalization" as of 2025.

### Gaps
- No published source giving exponential/biexponential insulin-action parameters fitted to Lyumjev or Fiasp for AID use was found (PK numbers above are from clamp/pump PK, not control models).
- No clinical validation of TDD-based CR (500 rule) in closed loop was found; the ISF evidence is a single pediatric cohort.
- Diabits and Tidepool "digital twin" approaches: no primary sources retrieved.

### Inferences
- The field's 2025–26 frontier splits into (i) foundation-model representations for risk/forecasting (not yet in loops), (ii) digital-twin-driven slow parameter adaptation (clinically validated), and (iii) RL controllers with hand-coded safety shells (in silico only). For a shippable open-source redesign, (ii) plus the zone-MPC safety mechanisms and an estimator-based meal detector is the evidence-aligned path; (i) and (iii) are research bets.
- The absence of any offline-RL-on-real-AID-data publication is itself a finding: large Nightscout/OpenAPS datasets exist but no retrieved 2025–26 paper trained a deployable policy on them.
