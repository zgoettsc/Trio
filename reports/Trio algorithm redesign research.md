# Keep oref1's guardrails, rebuild its brain

**Verdict: evolve oref1 by staged replacement, do not delete it; and the "80%+ TIR with zero user entry" claim attributed to Medtronic is not substantiated by any disclosed result.** Trio's dosing core (`trio-oref/lib/determine-basal/determine-basal.js`, 1,931 lines in this fork versus 1,643 upstream) is a purely reactive, single-scalar-adaptive controller whose rise detection is an implicit side-effect of the deviation term, whose per-cycle insulin ceiling is `basal × 30 min` at half of `insulinReq`, and whose only learning is a 0.7–1.2 sensitivity multiplier; the fork's 306-line "meal window / phantom COB" overlay treats these symptoms with multiplicative overrides rather than a better model. Replacing it wholesale with MPC, RL, or a from-scratch design is unjustified on the evidence: oref1 is the only DIY algorithm with RCT evidence (CREATE: **71.2% TIR**, 14-point adjusted gain, no severe hypoglycemia; [NEJM 2022](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2203913)), it beat Loop 58% to 35% TIR in the only head-to-head no-announcement comparison ([Lal et al. 2021](https://doaj.org/article/b08f806168ca4c878812280d3471a23a)), no RL controller has a clinical trial, and Trio has no simulator or dosing test to validate a greenfield rewrite. Meanwhile the ceiling the user is chasing does not exist yet in insulin-only type 1 systems: every announcement-free dataset lands at **45–68% TIR** (CamAPS HX in T1D 49.9%, Tandem Freedom 61%, Control-IQ no-bolus days 62%, iLet no-meal users ~62%, Insulet T2D FCL 68%), and the only ≥80% zero-input results are bihormonal ([Inreda, 80.3% at one year](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/landig/article/PIIS2589-7500(24)00002-5/fulltext)), community-reported (Boost ~87%), or in silico. Medtronic's Vivera has exactly two small feasibility cohorts in public and neither reaches 80% without announcements: the company's S-1 reports **73.8% TIR "without manual user input"** in the adult cohort (n=14), with **~82% when meals were announced** in the same study per its ADA 2026 poster, and no TBR or TAR ever disclosed for that cohort ([MiniMed S-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2062583/000162828025058231/minimedgroupincs-1.htm), [MedTech Dive, 9 Jun 2026](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/minimed-is-in-the-lead-as-diabetes-tech-firms-focus-on-fully-closed-loop/822446/)); the only per-arm table in the public domain, a pediatric slide (n=12) photographed at ATTD 2026, shows **64.7% TIR fully unannounced** (TBR <70 1.9%, time >250 mg/dL 11.3%) versus 73.7% on the same algorithm with announcements and 67.8% on the participants' own 780G ([ATTD 2026 slide photo](https://x.com/carogomezmartin/status/2032042408804843741)). No first-hand tester, employee or investigator account of ≥80% TIR with zero announcements exists anywhere in filings, transcripts, trade press or social media; every ≥80% figure in circulation is the announced-meals arm (82%), 780G users on optimal settings *with* meal boluses (78.8–81.9%), or type 2 cohorts, and the 389-person GATEWAY pivotal does not complete until roughly January 2027 ([ClinicalTrials.gov NCT07228117](https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies/NCT07228117)). Because this user will not tag meals, enter macros or announce carbs consistently, every mechanism recommended here runs on glucose, insulin history, wearables and time alone, with carb entries as an optional noisy prior; under that constraint the evidence supports a zero-input ceiling of **70–76% TIR** for insulin-only type 1 today, with personalization (~3–5 points in the UVA digital-twin RCT) and the controller changes as the path to push it upward, and a second hormone as the only demonstrated route above 80% with zero input. The plan is to wrap oref1 in an independent hypoglycemia supervisor, replace its sizing, rise-detection, absorption and learning sub-blocks one at a time behind a replay harness and shadow mode, and only then decide whether the residual oref1 core is worth keeping.

## Trio today is a reactive controller with a multiplicative overlay and no dosing tests

The loop is sound at the shell level and brittle at the core. Every 3 minutes at most (`Config.loopInterval`), `APSManager.determineBasal()` computes TDD, validates glucose (newest reading ≤12 minutes old, more than 2 readings in the last hour), refreshes autosens no more than every 30 minutes, assembles 13 JSON inputs, runs `meal → iob → determine-basal` in JavaScriptCore, stores a `Determination`, and `enactDetermination()` sends the temp basal and then the SMB (`Trio/Sources/APS/APSManager.swift:280-345, 437-552, 1682-1740`; `Trio/Sources/APS/OpenAPS/OpenAPS.swift:410-504`). That input-assembly and enactment shell is the part of Trio worth keeping unchanged in any redesign, because it is where pump history, carbs, glucose, the override state and the fork's `TrioCustomOrefVariables` already converge (`OpenAPS.swift:553-686`).

Inside the JS, rises are never detected, only inferred. The algorithm computes `delta` as the mean of 5-minute deltas 2.5–7.5 minutes back, `short_avgdelta` over 2.5–17.5 minutes and `long_avgdelta` over 17.5–42.5 minutes (`trio-oref/lib/glucose-get-last.js:25-88`), then `deviation = 6 × (minDelta − bgi)` where `minDelta = min(delta, short_avgdelta)` and negative deviations are re-damped with longer averages (`determine-basal.js:524-526, 809-819`). Carb impact `ci = minDelta − bgi` is capped at 30 g/h, un-entered carbs are assumed to absorb at 20 g/h with a triangular profile, the deviation is decayed to zero over 60 minutes in the IOB curve, and the UAM curve assumes deviations "drop back down at least at 1/3 the rate they ramped up" (`determine-basal.js:928-1003, 1041-1103`). **There is no acceleration, curvature or onset term anywhere in the dosing path**; the fork's 3-state Kalman filter (`[BG, velocity, acceleration]`, measurement noise 36) exists only in `Trio/Sources/APS/SignalProcessing/AdaptiveKalmanFilter.swift` and runs in shadow mode, "Phase 1: compute and log, no dosing changes" (`APSManager.swift:509-522`). Taking the minimum of 5- and 15-minute deltas and 45-minute slopes adds 10–45 minutes of lag before a meal registers, which is exactly where the field's meal-detection literature puts the cost: delay buys time-above-range, not safety ([systematic review of meal-announcement-free algorithms](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13349122/)).

Dosing is then structurally throttled. `insulinReq = (min(minPredBG, eventualBG) − target)/sens`, SMB size is `min(insulinReq × smb_delivery_ratio, maxBolus)` with `smb_delivery_ratio` 0.5 and `maxBolus = basal × 30/60 U`, one SMB per 3 minutes, and SMBs are disabled outright whenever `maxDelta > 0.2 × bg` (`determine-basal.js:1369-1380, 1612, 1744-1822`). The fork's own case study shows the consequence: an 80 g carb / 20 g fat / 50 g protein sushi meal had COB reach zero at ~148 minutes while glucose peaked at **307 mg/dL at 177 minutes**, with SMBs of 0.5–0.6 U against an `insulinReq` of 1.0–3.2 U (`docs/TOUGH_MEAL_HANDLING.md:1-60`). The fork answers this with a stack of overrides that compose multiplicatively: a meal window raises the SMB ratio to 0.8, doubles `maxSMBBasalMinutes`, floors `insulinReq` at 20–40% of `(bg − target)/sens` by delta, forces UAM, injects 20 g of phantom COB when `mealCOB < 15`, and a tough-meal state lifts `autosens_max` to 1.4 and multiplies the SMB cap 1.5×/2×/2.5× above 150/200/250 mg/dL (`determine-basal.js:136-234, 268-298, 1626-1684, 1736-1822`; `Trio/Sources/APS/MealClassifier.swift:86-300`; auto-injector `APSManager.swift:1276-1430`). With a 1 U/h basal, the in-window ceiling above 250 mg/dL is 0.5 × 2 × 2.5 = 2.5 U every 3 minutes, bounded only by 75% of `insulinReq` and `max_iob`; the code itself notes "Lying to oref is high-risk" (`determine-basal.js:194`). None of this is covered by automated tests: `TrioTests/` exercises storage, smoothing, TDD sufficiency and the manual-bolus validator, while `trio-oref/` has no `tests/` directory at all.

Adaptation is a single scalar. Autosens takes the 50th percentile of non-meal deviations over 8 and 24 hours and clamps the ratio to 0.7–1.2 (`trio-oref/lib/determine-basal/autosens.js:316-414`; `Trio/Resources/javascript/prepare/autosens.js:54-75`); Dynamic ISF, when on, overwrites that ratio with `sens × AF × TDD × ln(BG/insulinFactor + 1)/1800` and, unless `tddAdjBasal` is set, basal is no longer scaled at all (`determine-basal.js:407-455, 663-682`). CR, DIA, peak time and every absorption constant are never learned; autotune is bundled but dead, since `makeProfile` is always called with `autotune: RawJSON.null` and the UI module calls an `apsManager.autotune()` that does not exist (`OpenAPS.swift:804, 817`; `Trio/Sources/Modules/AutotuneConfig/AutotuneConfigStateModel.swift:56`). Two more defects matter for a redesign: the Swift glucose DTO never sends `noise`, `device` or calibration records, so every oref noise guard is inert (`OpenAPS.swift:172-178` vs `glucose-get-last.js:27-32`), and `weightPercentage` defaults to 0.35 in Swift but 0.65 in the JS profile (`Trio/Sources/Models/Preferences.swift:50` vs `trio-oref/lib/profile/index.js:77`). Finally, the fork is stranded: it runs the JS bundle on a v0.8.3 base with an unfinished merge (`MERGE_NOTES.md:1-55`), while upstream Trio v1.0 (15 Sep 2026) rewrote the algorithm in Swift with "the decisions themselves have not changed" and removed the JavaScript path ([nightscout/Trio releases](https://github.com/nightscout/Trio/releases)). Any new logic written into `determine-basal.js` will have to be ported twice.

Three readings follow from the table. First, the Medtronic benchmark decomposes cleanly: the 780G's "meal detection" is the PID reacting to SG >120 with basal saturated, and its auto-corrections are only **17.3% of TDD on average (range 3.4–41.3%)** in 235 real-world users, with users bolusing fewer than three times a day falling to 67.5% TIR ([Enciso Izquierdo 2024](https://www.elsevier.es/en-revista-endocrinologia-diabetes-nutricion-english-ed--413-pdf-download-S2530018024000702)). The no-bolus-day analysis (70.9% overall, 76.3% on 100 mg/dL target + 2-h AIT, N=369,467 users of whom 54,553 had ≥10 such days) is the strongest quantitative evidence anywhere of what a tuned, 5-minute-correcting insulin-only controller recovers from unannounced meals, and Medtronic itself labels it off-label ([Diabetes Care 2026](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/49/3/419/164061/MiniMed-780G-Advanced-Hybrid-Closed-Loop-System)). The CEO's own target for Vivera is "for type 1 above 70% time in range and for type 2, possibly higher" ([BofA transcript, 23 Sept 2026](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/minimed-at-bank-of-america-global-healthcare-conference-growth-push-93CH-4912616)); the 10-K states the feasibility protocols "were not designed to prove statistical significance" ([MiniMed 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0002062583/000162828026045915/mdt-20260424.htm)). So the honest benchmark is: Medtronic has a credible, patented mechanism (US 11,806,137: a 3-hour window of SG paired with model-estimated plasma insulin, logistic regression or LSTM, 91→96% accuracy when plasma insulin is added, acting by temporarily lowering the correction target and ISF so that a series of 5-minute corrections replaces the meal bolus, with any late user bolus debited by corrections already given; [Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11806137B2/en); its 2026 continuation US 12,629,472 adds that corrections may be temporarily enlarged, must respect the maximum basal rate and IOB, and that "if detection was wrong, further correction boluses can be stopped and basal delivery temporarily suspended" ([Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12629472B2/en))) and no published outcome above the mid-70s without announcements. The one per-arm table that exists sharpens the picture considerably: in 12 pediatric users on the same NMX7 algorithm, removing announcements cost **9 TIR points (73.7→64.7%)**, entirely as time above range (TAR >180 24.1→32.9%, >250 6.2→11.3%, mean SG 148.5→161.6 mg/dL) with TBR unchanged at 1.9%, while total daily insulin was flat (45.5 vs 46.7 U/d) and "announce at will" (1.1 announcements/day, ~40 g/day) recovered about a third of the gap at 67.9% ([ATTD 2026 slide](https://x.com/carogomezmartin/status/2032042408804843741)). Flat TDD with a TAR penalty is the signature of a reactive correction-based meal response that delivers the same insulin later, not a feed-forward automatic meal bolus; the adult cohort's 73.8% ([S-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2062583/000162828025058231/minimedgroupincs-1.htm)) versus ~82% announced ([MD+DI, BTIG note](https://www.mddionline.com/diabetes/minimed-emerges-as-the-only-full-stack-diabetes-tech-player-in-a-18b-market)) shows the same ~8-point gap. The company's own filing language ("not designed to prove statistical significance"; "heightened level of risk with respect to technical feasibility"), the absence of any Medtronic press release on the ATTD or ADA data, and management's refusal to say when pivotal data will be published ([Wells Fargo conference, 8 Sep 2026](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/mmed/transcripts/739622-wells-fargo-21st-annual-healthcare-conference/)) all point the same way: the "internal tester at 80%+ with zero announcements" story is most plausibly a garbled retelling of the 82%-announced arm, the 780G no-bolus-day 76–82% figures, or the single 13-year-old whose HbA1c fell from 8% to 6.7% in a mode that was never stated ([Diabetech, 12 Mar 2026](https://www.diabetech.info/p/minimed-unveils-next-gen-vivera-fully-closed-loop-algorithm)). Vivera's adult 73.8% is nonetheless at the top of the published insulin-only FCL range for type 1, which is itself the point: the best-resourced vendor, with a simulator of ~20,000 virtual patients, lands within a few points of what a 780G on optimal settings already does on a missed-bolus day.

Second, the second hormone is what moves zero-input TIR into the 80s. Inreda's 80.3% at one year with SD 5.4 against 55.5% baseline, and CamAPS HX's 66.3% in type 2 (residual endogenous insulin) versus 49.9% in C-peptide-negative type 1 with the same MPC and Lyumjev, show that counter-regulation, not controller cleverness, is the binding constraint on aggressive dosing into unannounced rises. Ultra-rapid insulin is worth only **+2.5 points** in hybrid use ([DTT 2023](https://boris.unibe.ch/187146/1/dia.2023.0262.pdf)) and a non-significant 49.3% vs 39.9% in a missed-bolus FCL test ([PMC12434446](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12434446/)). Trio cannot dose glucagon, so its design must substitute conservative, bounded aggression plus a coarse meal signal.

Third, the coarse-signal middle path is where most of the industry is aiming, and it is worth knowing what it is worth even though this user cannot be relied on to use it. Diabeloop's labels, iLet's Usual/More/Less, Medtrum's meal type, CamAPS's "Add Meal", Dana Lewis's "small, medium, or large" ([Beyond Type 1](https://beyondtype1.org/the-future-of-openaps-with-dana-lewis/)) and this fork's own `AnnounceMealIntentRequest` all converge on the same interface, and DBLG2's quantified 3–6-point penalty for dropping it, like Vivera's ~3-point at-will recovery, is the best estimate of what a one-tap signal buys. For a user who will not tap consistently, the design must treat that tap as an optional bonus and be scored on days with none; the detector, the controller and the personalization have to earn those points instead.

## Why wholesale replacement loses to staged evolution for every candidate replacement

Each "delete oref1 and use X" option was steelmanned against the evidence. **Zone-MPC** is the strongest candidate: it is the published lineage behind Omnipod 5, runs in ~1.2 s on a 2014 phone, and its asymmetric cost with velocity weighting cut time <70 mg/dL four-fold at equal TIR in silico ([Automatica 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6051553/)). But its personalization is a single TDI-scaled gain on a subject-independent linear model, exactly the "single scalar" limitation Trio already has, and its clinical outcomes as Omnipod 5 (73.9% pivotal, 67.8% real-world, 68% in T2D FCL) are no better than oref1's. The useful parts of zone-MPC are its cost-shaping ideas, which transplant into oref1's `insulinReq` and SMB sizing without a QP solver. **Loop-style single-forecast control** lost the only FCL head-to-head to oref1 (35% vs 58%) because it has no unannounced-meal curve, though its integral retrospective correction is a well-specified component worth taking. **CamAPS-style adaptive MPC** is closed-source and, without meal input in type 1, reaches 45–50%. **Reinforcement learning** reports 87% TIR fully closed in simglucose, but every such result sits on simplified meal patterns, needs >4,000 training episodes per virtual patient, wraps the policy in hand-written safety rules, and has no clinical trial ([PLOS ONE 2025](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0317662)); the absence of any 2025–26 paper training a deployable policy on real Nightscout/OpenAPS data is itself a finding. **A from-scratch design** would discard the one DIY algorithm with RCT evidence, the fork's working input/enactment shell, and years of field-tested edge cases (orphaned resumes, temp mismatches, stale CGM handling) with no simulator in the repo to catch regressions. Medtronic's own path is the clearest counter-example to "start fresh": although marketing calls Vivera "built entirely from the ground up" ([Diabetech, 12 Mar 2026](https://www.diabetech.info/p/minimed-unveils-next-gen-vivera-fully-closed-loop-algorithm)), everything disclosed (TDD-seeded start replacing the 48-hour learning window, the same 5-minute correction machinery with a lower attainable target, the meal-detection classifier promoted from missed-bolus backup to primary meal path by modulating the correction target and ISF, removal of the SmartGuard exits, and in-silico re-tuning on ~20,000 virtual patients) describes the 780G PID-with-corrections controller wrapped in new detection, adaptation and safety behaviour, run as NMX7 on unmodified 780G hardware for its feasibility studies ([ANZCTR registry](https://anzctr.org.au/Trial/Registration/TrialReview.aspx?ACTRN=12623001076662p), [US 12,629,472](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12629472B2/en), [MedTech Dive, 9 Jun 2026](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/minimed-is-in-the-lead-as-diabetes-tech-firms-focus-on-fully-closed-loop/822446/)). That is exactly the strangler pattern recommended here, executed by the vendor with the most resources and the most installed-base data.

The decisive argument is procedural rather than algorithmic. Boost, the only open-source project with multi-user ~87% TIR fully closed loop, got there by keeping "the AndroidAPS engine (basal, dynamic sensitivity, predictions, and all safety gates)" and replacing "only the decision about the size of the next micro-bolus," running "thirteen components in shadow mode until they are scored and promoted or dropped," and constraining learned components so one "can only reduce a dose" and the other "is the only learned component allowed to add insulin" ([diabettech](https://www.diabettech.com/beyond-carb-counting-boosts-evolution-and-the-limits-of-fully-closed-loop/)). The failures it reports are instructive: a rise-outcome estimator, an anticipatory backout, and a plateau detector that "flagged highs already resolving" were all dropped after scoring. Trio's fork already has the right shape for this process (the `OrefSignalPipeline` shadow mode and `AlgorithmTelemetryManager`), but has used it only for logging while the live changes went straight into JS as "HOTFIX" and "round-N" commits. Evolving oref1 behind a harness is therefore not the timid option; it is the only path that has produced the user's target number in the open-source world.

## Top 10 changes ranked by expected impact per unit of risk

The ranking puts risk-reducing and enabling changes first, then the rise-and-dosing changes whose aggression they bound, then model and learning changes with longer validation horizons. Each item names the mechanism, the system or paper that proves it, the expected effect on rises, dosing and hypoglycemia, the safety considerations, and the code it replaces or extends. "Swift" placement is deliberate: new modules should live beside `Trio/Sources/APS/SignalProcessing/` and talk to the core through the existing seam (`TrioCustomOrefVariables` → `trio_custom_oref_variables.json` in, `monitor/iob.json` and `OrefDetermination` out), because that seam survives the pending rebase onto upstream's Swift oref, whereas edits to `determine-basal.js` will not.

### 1. Build the replay harness and make shadow-mode promotion the only path to live dosing

**What and mechanism.** A deterministic replay runner that takes Trio's stored inputs (the 13 JSON inputs already assembled in `OpenAPS.determineBasal`, the `OrefDetermination` and `Forecast` rows in Core Data) and re-runs any candidate controller over weeks of the user's own history, plus a personal digital twin (ReplayBG/PyReplayBG fits a personalized model from retrospective T1D data; simglucose and its 2026 extension add counter-regulation and activity; T1DMSIM benchmarks against OhioT1DM/ShanghaiT1DM) for counterfactual dosing, plus a promotion rule that no component may dose until it has been scored in shadow ([ReplayBG](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/371911244_ReplayBG_a_digital_twin-based_methodology_to_identify_a_personalized_model_from_type_1_diabetes_data_and_simulate_glucose_concentrations_to_assess_alternative_therapies), [simglucose](https://pypi.org/project/simglucose/0.1.5/), [Extended Simglucose 2026](https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2026/151886/151886.pdf), [T1DMSIM](https://github.com/0xdeadf1sh/T1DMSIM)). LoopKit's `LoopAlgorithmRunner` scenario-JSON CLI is the closest DIY precedent for the interface ([LoopKit/LoopAlgorithm](https://github.com/LoopKit/LoopAlgorithm)). **Proven by** Boost's shadow-and-score process and by FDA's acceptance of a 125-twin × 72-scenario in-silico trial plus real-world extrapolation as the sole evidence for Omnipod 5's December 2025 target change ([K251779](https://fda.innolitics.com/device/K251779)). **Effect** on rises/dosing/hypos is indirect but it is the precondition for every item below being measured rather than guessed. **Safety** is strictly positive. **Maps to** `Trio/Sources/APS/SignalProcessing/OrefSignalPipeline.swift:5-13, 150-245` (shadow infrastructure already exists), `APSManager.swift:546-552, 788` (telemetry only during meal windows today; must log every loop), `OpenAPS.swift:61-113` (stored determinations), and a new `trio-oref/tests/` or Swift test target replaying the fork's own "20 bad meals".

### 2. Install an independent hypoglycemia supervisor that can only subtract insulin

**What and mechanism.** A separate Swift module that runs after whatever controller produced the proposal and may only reduce it, composed of: a 30-minute linear-regression predictive suspend that zero-temps when predicted BG <80 mg/dL or current <70; a velocity-weighted correction gate that scales positive corrections to zero once BG is falling at ≥1 mg/dL/min and ramps in between; a hard IOB ceiling on SMBs (autoISF's `iobTHvirtual`, a percentage of `max_iob` with 30% tolerance); rolling delivery caps per hour and per meal; 780G-style anti-windup exits (basal saturated for 7 hours or minimal for 3–6 hours triggers a fallback mode); a CGM quality gate using the noise, device and calibration fields the Swift DTO currently drops; and insulin-specific onset timing replacing the hard-coded 90-minute wait before `minIOBPredBG`/`minCOBPredBG` count. **Proven by** PROLOG (PLGS cut median time <70 from 3.6% to 2.6% vs 3.2% SAP with mean glucose unchanged at 159 mg/dL; [Diabetes Care 2018](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/41/10/2155/36689/Predictive-Low-Glucose-Suspend-Reduces)), zone-MPC velocity weighting (`Q(v)=ε` for v ≤ −1 mg/dL/min; <70 0.51% vs 2.02%, ≥60-min suspensions 318 vs 761; [Automatica 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6051553/)), the 780G's predicted-low subtraction from corrections and its 7-hour max-basal exit ([AGDT fact sheet](https://www.diabetesnet.com/pdfs/MiniMed-780G-with-SmartGuard.pdf)), and Boost's "absolute time-below-range floors that can only tighten." **Effect:** no direct effect on rises; permits every later item to be more aggressive at bounded hypo cost; expected to remove the post-SMB tail lows that oref1's zero-temp pairing only partly prevents. **Safety:** this is the safety layer; its own risk is over-suspension causing rebound highs, which the harness must measure (suspension count and duration, as the zone-MPC paper does). **Maps to** `determine-basal.js:1407-1430` (current predictive LGS, 30–120 min), `:1358-1366` (SMB off if `minGuardBG < threshold`), `:1116-1128` (90-minute wait "regardless of insulin type"), `:534-579` (inert noise branch), `OpenAPS.swift:172-178` (add noise/device/cal to the glucose DTO), `trio-oref/lib/iob/calculate.js:81-141` (peak 75/55; Fiasp peak ~63 min, Lyumjev onset 10 min faster and 44 min shorter than Humalog per [PMC8603316](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8603316/) and [NCT05403502 protocol](https://cdn.clinicaltrials.gov/large-docs/02/NCT05403502/Prot_000.pdf)).

### 3. Replace fixed 50%/30-basal-minute SMB sizing with a glucose- and velocity-ranged fraction under model-based caps, and retire the fork's multiplicative stack

**What and mechanism.** Deliver a fraction of `insulinReq` that rises with distance above target and with rising velocity (autoISF: `new_SMB = lower + (higher − lower) × (bg − target)/range`; Loop's Glucose Based Partial Application 20–80%), cap each SMB and each rolling hour by a TDD-anchored limit rather than `basal × 30 min` (Omnipod: automated delivery ≤4× an Adaptive Rate ≈ 0.5 × decaying TDI, ≤12 corrections/h; iLet: 3 U per automatic correction; Control-IQ: 60% of the correction, ≤1/h), and delete the meal-window ratio 0.8, 2× minutes multiplier, tough-meal 1.5–2.5× cap and `insulinReq` floor in favour of this single, monotone sizing function. **Proven by** two independent lineages converging ([autoISF plugin](https://github.com/T-o-b-i-a-s/AndroidAPS/blob/3.4.2.6+aisf3.2.1/plugins/aps/src/main/kotlin/app/aaps/plugins/aps/openAPSAutoISF/OpenAPSAutoISFPlugin.kt), [LoopDocs](https://loopkit.github.io/loopdocs/operation/algorithm/auto-adjust/)) and by the three cleared commercial sizing rules above ([Omnipod HCP page](https://www.omnipod.com/hcp/clinical-resources/provider-resources/omnipod-5-algorithm), [K220916](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf22/K220916.pdf), [Tandem](https://tdcqawww.apos.tandemdiabetes.com/providers/products/control-iq)). **Effect:** directly addresses the sushi case (0.5–0.6 U SMBs against 1.0–3.2 U required); larger fractions only when high and rising, smaller when near target or decelerating, so dosing becomes faster on real rises without raising late-stacking risk. **Safety:** the ranged fraction must be bounded by item 2's IOB ceiling and velocity gate; STRIVE's +3 TIR for +0.3 TBR when Omnipod 6 added "up to 50% more automated insulin" is the realistic exchange rate to expect and test against. **Maps to** `determine-basal.js:1744-1822` (maxBolus, `smb_delivery_ratio`), `:1626-1684` (meal-window `insulinReq` floor), `:1736-1742, 1774-1786, 1803-1820` (multipliers), `Preferences.swift:4-54` (`smbDeliveryRatio 0.5`, `maxSMBBasalMinutes 30`), `TrioSettings.swift:83-129` (window state).

### 4. Detect rises with an acceleration state and gate the SMB lockout on it, instead of waiting for damped deltas

**What and mechanism.** Promote the shadow Kalman filter's `[BG, velocity, acceleration]` state into the dosing path: use filtered velocity and acceleration (or autoISF's quadratic fit with correlation ≥0.9, extrapolated to the vertex) to (a) relax the `maxDelta > 0.2 × bg` SMB lockout to 0.3 only when acceleration is positive and the fit is confident, (b) add an acceleration-driven ISF lift and a deceleration brake (autoISF `acce_ISF`, braking when the vertex is predicted within 30 minutes and below target), and (c) apply zone-MPC's "velocity penalty" idea, an extra push only in the 140–180 band at the start of a rise. **Proven by** autoISF's mechanics and Tai's demonstration that it integrates into Trio's Swift oref through one `autoISFStatus` argument ([mountrcg/Tai](https://github.com/mountrcg/Tai), [Tai PR #19](https://github.com/mountrcg/Tai/pull/19)), Boost's accelerating-meal detector (40.6% of fires followed by a ≥1.7 mmol/L rise within 45 minutes vs 19.6% background over 154,000 cycles), and zone-MPC's velocity penalty `D=1000` for BG ∈ [140,180] ([Automatica 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6051553/)). **Effect:** removes the paradox that oref1 withholds SMBs during the steepest rises and cuts 10–45 minutes of delta-averaging lag; the brake side reduces overshoot lows after a peak. **Safety:** no outcome data exist for autoISF; the acceleration term must be the only thing that lifts the lockout, calibration jumps must still block (use the new `cal` records), and the brake must be allowed to act even when the lift is disabled. **Maps to** `AdaptiveKalmanFilter.swift:1-38`, `glucose-get-last.js:25-88` (delta definitions; upstream Trio removed autoISF fields here, commit `4204b12`), `determine-basal.js:1369-1380` (lockout), `:809-819` (deviation damping), `:999-1003` (1/3 slope assumption).

### 5. Learn basal, ISF and CR per time block with a slow run-to-run loop and a periodic digital-twin refit, replacing the single autosens scalar and the dead autotune

**What and mechanism.** A nightly or bi-weekly learner that estimates the basal profile by time block, ISF by time block, and CR from detected meal episodes and the insulin that normalized them (never from entered carbs, which this user does not supply consistently), all from the stored glucose and insulin history, moving each at most a bounded fraction per run (autotune's 20% per run is a reasonable prior), anchored to a TDD prior (ISF ≈ 1800/TDD; pediatric fit 1736/TDD, [Frontiers in Pediatrics 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/pediatrics/articles/10.3389/fped.2022.854972/full)), excluding periods under temporary overrides, Boost/Ease-off-style modes and the first weeks after a profile change, and periodically refit against a personal twin. **Proven by** the UVA digital-twin RCT (n=72, bi-weekly re-optimization, **72→77% TIR**, HbA1c 6.8→6.6%; [npj Digital Medicine 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-025-01679-y)), CamAPS's 1–3-week learning with overrides "excluded from learning" ([Steckbrief 2026](https://diabetes-technologie.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Steckbrief_AID_CamAPS-FX_final_022026.pdf)), the 780G's 2–6-day TDD gain adaptation, Omnipod's decaying-TDI Adaptive Rate, iLet's weight-only initialization, and in-silico R2R/ILC gains of 2–4 TIR points over 4–17 weeks ([arXiv 2606.17004](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.17004), [IEEE TBME 2020](https://doi.org/10.1109/tbme.2020.3005622)). **Effect:** the single biggest reduction in user input (basal/ISF/CR maintenance disappears), corrects the attribution error where a wrong CR is absorbed as "sensitivity" by autosens, and delivers the time-of-day memory requested in Trio issue #1516 ([nightscout/Trio #1516](https://github.com/nightscout/Trio/issues/1516)) by construction. **Safety:** slow timescale and bounded steps make it low-risk, but it must ignore UAM-categorized deviations when tuning basal (the known autotune inflation failure, [AAPS Autotune docs](https://androidaps.readthedocs.io/en/latest/AdvancedOptions/Autotune.html)) and must not learn from phantom-COB or override periods. **Maps to** `autosens.js:147-414`, `trio-oref/lib/autotune/index.js:238-512` (dead code to mine, then delete), `OpenAPS.swift:804, 817` (`autotune: RawJSON.null`), `AutotuneConfigStateModel.swift:56`, `Trio/Sources/APS/Storage/TDDStorage.swift:607-636`, `SignalProcessing/AdaptiveLearning.swift:1-34` (EWMA scaffold already exists), `ProfileManager/TherapyProfiles` (scheduled profile switching).

### 6. Replace phantom COB with an estimator-based meal detector whose action is a temporary target/ISF shift, plus an optional one-tap meal signal

**What and mechanism.** Run a disturbance estimator on the residual between observed and insulin-explained glucose (the fork's `BGResidualCalculator` is the seed) with a CUSUM or logistic-regression decision over a trailing window that includes a fasting baseline, as in Medtronic's patent (3-hour window, SG paired with model-estimated plasma insulin normalized by half the 6-day median TDD, threshold ~50% default, 70–90% for confidence). On detection, do what the patent family and the 780G do rather than what the fork does: temporarily lower the correction target and ISF so that the existing correction machinery issues a series of bounded 5-minute corrections (the 2026 continuation also allows the corrections to be temporarily enlarged, but only while "the maximum basal rate has [not] been reached" and IOB is below the correction amount, and capped "using either the current or a maximum glucose value, whichever is lower"), debit any later user bolus by corrections already delivered, wind down through an explicit "Recovering" state after the peak (Boost), and, critically, implement the patent's false-positive rollback: if the detection was wrong, "further correction boluses can be stopped and basal delivery temporarily suspended" ([US 12,629,472](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12629472B2/en)). The fork's one-tap "I'm eating" may remain as an optional coarse prior that raises detector sensitivity for a bounded window, but it is not load-bearing: this user will not announce consistently, so the detector carries the entire meal-handling burden and the system must perform identically on days with zero taps. **Proven by** the systematic review's envelope (median 88% sensitivity, 93% precision, 25–40 min delay; estimator methods such as Mahmoudi's KF+CUSUM 80% sensitivity at 100% precision and Sala-Mira's disturbance observer 90.5%/97.4% at 0.1 FP/day in silico; in vivo Mosquera-Lopez 83% sensitivity, 1.0 FP/day, 25.9 min, TIR +9.1 points with stable TBR; [PMC13349122](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13349122/)), US 11,806,137 ([Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11806137B2/en)), and DBLG2's 3–6-point cost for dropping announcements as the value of the optional tap ([PMLive](https://pmlive.com/pharma_news/diabeloops-dblg2-receives-fda-and-ce-clearance-for-automated-insulin-delivery-without-meal-input)). **Effect:** turns the fork's heuristic 3-phase classifier and COB fabrication into a probabilistic detector with a bounded action; the target-shift mechanism cannot "enable SMBs and raise predictions simultaneously" the way injected grams do. The expected ceiling should be set by Medtronic's per-arm data, which is the only field measurement of this exact mechanism: with the detector as the sole meal path, total daily insulin did not change (45.5 vs 46.7 U/d) and the cost of no announcements was ~9 TIR points taken wholly from time above range with TBR flat at 1.9% ([ATTD 2026 slide](https://x.com/carogomezmartin/status/2032042408804843741)), so the detector mainly shortens the time between a rise and the first adequate correction; it does not front-load insulin and will not, on its own, close the gap to announced-meal performance. For this user that gap is the honest expected cost: the detector-only path lands in the 65–74% band Vivera and the 780G no-bolus days show, and what pushes it upward is not announcements (worth ~3 points in the Vivera at-will arm and 3–6 points per DBLG2, but unavailable here) but items 3 and 4, which govern how large and how early the first corrections can be once the detector fires, item 7, which keeps the prediction lifted while the rise persists, and the personalization in the preceding section, which makes the corrections the right size for this body. **Safety:** false positives are the hypo risk and in-silico FP rates understate real ones (ensemble detector: 0.05 FP/day in silico vs 0.17 on OhioT1DM; [Computers in Biology and Medicine 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0010482524002385)), so tune on the user's own Nightscout data, require ≤0.5 FP/day in replay before promotion, size the action by item 2's supervisor, and handle rescue carbs and exercise as confounders (the fork already hides rescue-tagged carbs from oref, `OpenAPS.swift:186-196`). **Maps to** `MealClassifier.swift:86-300`, `APSManager.swift:1276-1430` (auto-injector, per-loop 8 g cap, per-window 200 g), `determine-basal.js:191-234` (phantom COB), `SignalProcessing/BGResidualCalculator.swift:1-33`, `Shortcuts/Meal/AnnounceMealIntentRequest.swift:108`.

### 7. Add a bounded persistent-error integrator (Loop IRC) in place of the fixed 60-minute deviation decay and the 1/3-slope UAM assumption

**What and mechanism.** Replace oref1's `predDev = ci × (1 − n/12)` decay and the UAM curve's `uci + n × slopeFromDeviations` extrapolation with Loop's integral retrospective correction: accumulate a contiguous run of same-sign discrepancies over a 180-minute window with `integralForget = exp(−5/60)`, `persistentDiscrepancyGain 2.0`, `currentDiscrepancyGain 1.0`, a differential term applied only when negative "to avoid upward momentum stacking", effect duration growing 10 minutes per discrepancy to a 180-minute cap, and a velocity clamp of 4 mg/dL/min; reset on sign change ([IntegralRetrospectiveCorrection.swift](https://github.com/LoopKit/LoopAlgorithm/blob/main/Sources/LoopAlgorithm/RetrospectiveCorrection/IntegralRetrospectiveCorrection.swift)). **Proven by** its specification and field use as an opt-in Loop experiment; it has no controlled outcome data, which is why it ranks here. **Effect:** persistent unmodeled rises (slow fat/protein, resistance, bad site) keep lifting the prediction in a principled way instead of being forgotten after an hour, so sustained highs get corrected earlier; the sign reset and negative-only differential keep it from stacking on the way down. **Safety:** the Loop reviewers' warning applies verbatim, overestimated carbs absorbing fast would make an integral term "more aggressive up front and more likely low on the tail end" ([Loop PR #726](https://github.com/LoopKit/Loop/pull/726)), so the integrator must be clamped, subordinate to item 2, and suppressed when the fork's carb estimate is above a limit. **Maps to** `determine-basal.js:1041-1103` (prediction loop), `:999-1003`, `cob.js:146-190` (slope computation).

### 8. Replace linear COB decay with observed-absorption estimation and a detector-selected absorption-class library

**What and mechanism.** Replace `min_5m_carbimpact 8`, the 20 g/h floor, the 30 g/h cap, the 90 g cap and the 6-hour window with absorption inferred from what glucose actually did: Loop's insulin counteraction effect (`AC = ICE × CR/ISF`; [LoopDocs](https://loopkit.github.io/loopdocs/operation/algorithm/prediction/)) as computed by the fork's shadow `BGResidualCalculator`, with the expiry floor tied to elapsed time since the detected onset rather than a fixed 24 g/h, and the 30 g/h cap raised or removed so fast absorption is tracked. Fat and protein become a slow disturbance class rather than FPU pseudo-carbs, but the class is chosen by the detector from a prior library (the shadow `MacroMealModel` curves, protein 120/210/360 min and fat 180/300/480 min, plus classes clustered from this user's own detected episodes) by matching rise shape and time of day; nothing is ever requested from the user, and any carb entry that exists only nudges the class choice. **Proven by** Loop's non-linear absorption since Loop 2.0, by the fork's own sushi case, where COB hit zero 30 minutes before the 307 mg/dL peak, and by the carb-free learning precedents in the preceding section. **Effect:** fewer premature "COB expired, stop dosing" decisions during slow meals and fewer phantom-COB workarounds; modest effect on fast rises, which items 4 and 7 handle. **Safety:** a slower-decaying absorption estimate is a longer-lived license to dose, so it must be bounded by the same IOB ceiling, and the estimate should feed predictions but never by itself enable SMBs (break the `enableSMB_with_COB` coupling). It ranks below the integrator because the integrator already captures much of the same persistent effect with fewer assumptions. **Maps to** `cob.js:52-106, 185-189`, `trio-oref/lib/meal/total.js:50-137`, `determine-basal.js:938-995`, `SignalProcessing/BGResidualCalculator.swift:1-33`, `SignalProcessing/MacroMealModel.swift:1-40`, `profile/index.js:27-34`.

### 9. Repair Dynamic ISF: AAPS-style TDD blend, high-TDD ceiling, consistent basal scaling, and no over-bolusing on rises

**What and mechanism.** Rebase the logarithmic formula on AAPS's blend (one-third each of 7-day average, previous day, and last 8 hours extrapolated), raise the 150% ceiling that "is unlikely to work well for anyone with a TDD of > 75u/day" (AAPS allows 300%), unify the 0.35/0.65 `weightPercentage` mismatch, scale basal and ISF by the same ratio instead of leaving basal unscaled when dynISF is on, and when BG is rising use a blend of current and predicted glucose for the ISF rather than eventual BG alone, since the latter "could produce over-large boluses while glucose was rising" ([Trio issue #1075](https://github.com/nightscout/Trio/issues/1075), [AAPS Dynamic ISF docs](https://androidaps.readthedocs.io/en/latest/DailyLifeWithAaps/DynamicISF.html), [diabettech](https://www.diabettech.com/automating-isf-in-open-source-aid-systems-experiments-with-androidaps/)). Also fix the cadence bug that silently disables dynISF when G7 readings arrive late ([Trio issue #1610](https://github.com/nightscout/Trio/issues/1610)). **Proven by** maintainer acknowledgement that the formula "will be revisited after the port" and by the 1736/TDD empirical fit. **Effect:** modest, mostly for high-TDD users and for consistency; it ranks here because it is low-risk housekeeping that item 5 partly supersedes. **Safety:** raising the ceiling raises aggression at high BG; keep the sigmoid's documented 150% caution ([TrioDocs](https://triodocs.org/configuration/settings/algorithm/dynamic-settings/)). **Maps to** `determine-basal.js:388-455, 663-682`, `Preferences.swift:45-50`, `profile/index.js:77`, `OpenAPS.swift:574-587`.

### 10. Wire heart rate and steps into the controller automatically for exercise and meal-detection confounding

**What and mechanism.** Feed Garmin/HealthKit heart rate and step count directly into the supervisor and detector: automatic insulin withdrawal and a raised target during detected aerobic exercise, a post-exercise sensitivity increase, a sleep detector that selects the overnight target, and heart rate as a second channel for the meal detector. **Proven by** Breton 2014 (HR-informed control-to-range significantly reduced BG decline during exercise, p=0.022; [PubMed 24702135](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24702135/)), the 2026 CGM+HR detector (median 28-minute detection at ~0.5 FP/day versus ~3.3 FP/day for rate-of-change alone; [Frontiers 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/digital-health/articles/10.3389/fdgth.2026.1758474/full)), and Boost's HR/step sleep detector and step-based withdrawal; the cautionary result is Jacobs 2015, where manually triggered exercise detection gave no benefit, so the wiring must be automatic ([JDST 2015](https://europepmc.org/api/getPdf?pmcid=PMC4667295)). **Effect:** mainly hypoglycemia prevention around activity, the failure mode Boost's author calls "a major failure point", and a roughly six-fold FP reduction for item 6. **Safety:** wearable data drop-outs must fail safe (no signal = no withdrawal and no detector bonus). It ranks last only because it depends on items 2 and 6 existing. **Maps to** `Trio/Sources/Services/SmartSense/SmartSenseManager.swift:5-16, 101` (currently UI-only, no file under `APS/OpenAPS` references it), `SignalProcessing/ExerciseSensitivityModel.swift:1-30` (shadow), `determine-basal.js:613-640` (temp-target-only exercise handling).

## Learning this user's body without relying on carb entry

A hard constraint governs everything above and below: this user does not tag meals, enter macronutrients, or announce carbs consistently, and will not start. Every learning, absorption and detection mechanism in this report therefore has to run on data that exists regardless of meal entry: CGM glucose, every insulin delivery (boluses, temp basals, SMBs and the TDD history already persisted in `TDDStored`), pump-site age, Garmin heart rate, steps, sleep and activity, HealthKit excluding nutrition, time of day and weekday, overrides, and whatever carb entries happen to exist, treated as an occasional noisy prior and never as a requirement. This section works through the three places where that constraint bites hardest: carb absorption, the case for a second hormone, and what can be learned about this user's physiology from their own data alone.

### A. Carb absorption: why oref is wrong in both directions, and what replaces it when carbs are not entered

The user observes absorption both far slower and far faster than oref expects because oref's COB is not an estimate of absorption at all; it is a bookkeeping entry forced to decay at a fixed minimum rate. In `cob.js`, each 5-minute bucket's carb impact is `ci = max(deviation, currentDeviation/2, min_5m_carbimpact)` and `absorbed = ci × carb_ratio / sens`, so COB falls by at least `min_5m_carbimpact` (default 8 mg/dL per 5 minutes, which the profile comments gloss as "24 g/hr at a CSF of 4") whether or not glucose is moving (`trio-oref/lib/determine-basal/cob.js:146-190`; `trio-oref/lib/profile/index.js:27-34`). At that floor a 60 g slow meal is erased from COB in roughly 2.5 hours regardless of what glucose does, which is exactly the fork's sushi case: 80 g carbohydrate with 20 g fat and 50 g protein had **COB reach zero at ~148 minutes while glucose was still climbing to 307 mg/dL at 177 minutes** (`docs/TOUGH_MEAL_HANDLING.md:1-60`). On the fast side, observed carb impact is capped at `maxCarbAbsorptionRate` of 30 g/h times CSF, so a rise faster than 30 g/h is partly attributed to nothing and under-predicted (`determine-basal.js:928-944`); un-absorbed carbs are then assumed to absorb at a minimum of 20 g/h on a triangular profile over a `remainingCATime` that starts at 3 hours divided by the sensitivity ratio, capped by a 90 g `remainingCarbsCap`, inside a 6-hour `maxMealAbsorptionTime` window, with the deviation term decayed to zero over 60 minutes in the IOB curve (`determine-basal.js:945-995, 1041-1050`; `trio-oref/lib/meal/total.js:50-137`). Nothing in the dosing path models fat or protein; FPU entries are simply more carbs to oref, and the fork's `MacroMealModel` (protein onset/peak/duration 120/210/360 minutes, fat 180/300/480 minutes) runs in shadow only (`Trio/Sources/APS/SignalProcessing/MacroMealModel.swift:1-40`). Upstream documentation confirms the intent rather than contradicting it: "Meals of 60g or less are assumed to absorb over about 3 hours" and carbs are capped at 90 g ([OpenAPS docs](https://openaps.readthedocs.io/en/latest/docs/While%20You%20Wait%20For%20Gear/Understand-determine-basal.html)). For a user who enters carbs rarely, these constants mostly act on nothing; when a carb entry does exist, they act on it wrongly.

Under the constraint, the alternatives rank as follows. **First and primary: make dosing independent of COB correctness.** Items 4, 6 and 7 together do this: an acceleration state detects the rise without a carb model, a bounded persistent-error integrator keeps lifting the prediction while the unexplained rise persists, and an estimator-based disturbance detector flags the meal and shifts the correction target. This is the lever every announcement-free system uses: CamAPS HX runs its MPC with no meal model at all ([Nat Med 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9873557/)), Medtronic's detector works on glucose paired with model-estimated plasma insulin and nothing else ([US 12,629,472](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12629472B2/en)), and Boost's meal hypothesis machine carries meal confidence across cycles without a gram entered ([diabettech](https://www.diabettech.com/beyond-carb-counting-boosts-evolution-and-the-limits-of-fully-closed-loop/)). **Second: replace the fixed-rate decay with an observed-absorption estimator.** Loop's insulin counteraction effect, `ICE = ΔBG_observed − ΔBG_insulin`, converted to grams by `CR/ISF`, infers absorption from what glucose actually did ([LoopDocs prediction](https://loopkit.github.io/loopdocs/operation/algorithm/prediction/)); the fork's shadow `BGResidualCalculator` computes the same residual with an EMA of 0.3 and a carb rate of `(dResidual/dt)/ISF × CR` (`Trio/Sources/APS/SignalProcessing/BGResidualCalculator.swift:1-33`). The expiry floor should be tied to elapsed time since the detected onset rather than a fixed 24 g/h, and the 30 g/h cap should be raised or removed so that fast absorption is tracked instead of truncated; the IOB ceiling in item 2 is what makes that safe. **Third: learn absorption from detected episodes, unsupervised.** Once the detector marks onsets, each episode has a time of day, a rise shape, a duration and a cumulative residual; clustering those across weeks yields this user's own absorption classes (a fast breakfast class, a slow late-dinner class) without anyone typing fat grams, and the `MacroMealModel` becomes a prior library the detector selects from by shape, not something the user feeds. Boost's accelerating-meal detector statistics (fires on 8.8% of cycles, 40.6% followed by a ≥1.7 mmol/L rise within 45 minutes versus 19.6% background) are an example of exactly this kind of episode-level, label-free scoring ([diabettech](https://www.diabettech.com/beyond-carb-counting-boosts-evolution-and-the-limits-of-fully-closed-loop/)). **Fourth: treat any carb entry that exists as a weak prior only**, raising detector sensitivity and seeding the absorption-class choice, but never gating SMB enablement (`enableSMB_with_COB`) or the prediction floor. Two things that look attractive on paper are explicitly not viable for this user: Loop's per-entry absorption time, which asks for a duration with every meal, and any per-tag learning (CamAPS learns "from previous insulin delivery and meal bolus information", iLet learns dose size per announced meal type; [Diabetesnet](https://www.diabetesnet.com/?p=8550), [FDA K220916](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf22/K220916.pdf)), because both require the consistent labelling the user will not supply.

### B. A second hormone is the only demonstrated route above 80% TIR with zero input

The evidence is unambiguous about where the ceiling moves. Inreda's bihormonal (insulin plus glucagon) fully closed loop, with no meal or exercise input, reached a **median 86.6% TIR versus 53.9% open loop in a two-week crossover RCT with time in hypoglycemia 0.4% versus 2%** ([Diabetes Care 2021](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/44/3/836/138610/Fully-Closed-Loop-Glucose-Control-With-a)), and in 71 Dutch real-world users over one year a **mean 80.3% TIR with an SD of only 5.4, median TBR 1.36%**, from a 55.5% baseline, with five SAEs including two severe hypoglycemic events ([Lancet Digital Health 2024](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/landig/article/PIIS2589-7500(24)00002-5/fulltext)). Every insulin-only no-input system in the comparison table sits at 45–68%. The mechanism is not subtle: glucagon lets the controller dose insulin aggressively into a rise because the hypoglycemia penalty for being wrong is recoverable, and the small SD shows it also collapses inter-person variance. Pramlintide addresses the opposite end, the rise rather than the low: McGill's multihormonal insulin-plus-pramlintide-plus-glucagon system cut TBR to 0.93% from 4.7% insulin-only at similar TIR (81% vs 83.3%) in nine in-vivo subjects with 30–40 minute detection delays ([systematic review table](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13349122/)), and the Lancet Digital Health 2024 crossover found insulin plus pramlintide with simple meal announcements non-inferior to insulin with carb counting, at the cost of mild GI symptoms in 47% and moderate in 7% ([Lancet Digital Health 2024](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/landig/article/PIIS2589-7500(24)00092-X/fulltext)); the lispro-aabc plus pramlintide fully-closed trial reported 71.1–71.2% versus 72.1% at three weeks, with benefit confined to those starting below 70% TIR ([Healio, July 2026](https://www.healio.com/news/endocrinology/20260706/fully-closedloop-system-with-insulin-lispro-plus-pramlintide-may-benefit-type-1-diabetes)). Pramlintide is relevant to this user's rise problem; its only conflict with the constraint is that the McGill protocols used simple meal announcements, so its FCL performance without any announcement is undemonstrated. No commercial bihormonal product exists outside the Netherlands: Beta Bionics' bihormonal iLet is at Phase 2a with Xeris as glucagon partner and "requires separate pump and algorithm 510(k)s plus an NDA for the glucagon product" ([Beta Bionics 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001674632/000119312526392088/d153052d8k.htm)); Zucara's ZT-01, a once-nightly SSTR2 antagonist meant to restore endogenous glucagon counter-regulation, is in Phase 2a ([Hub71](https://explorer.hub71.com/news/feed/zucara-therapeutics-closes-25m-series-b)).

For Trio, which already drives one Omnipod, the question is whether a second pod could dispense a glucose-raising agent under algorithm control. The research notes do not cover formulation chemistry or device compatibility, so the following is stated qualitatively and must be verified before any design work: glucagon is the only realistic glucose-raising agent (subcutaneous dextrose is not viable at pump volumes); native glucagon is unstable in solution, which is why Inreda's design depends on frequent refills and why stabilized liquid formulations (Xeris' and dasiglucagon-type products) are the only candidates for a reservoir; none is labelled for pump delivery, compatibility with Omnipod reservoir plastics and occlusion behaviour are unknown, and off-label drug delivery through a repurposed device is a different risk class from software changes to insulin dosing. The pharmacology also imposes controller rules: repeated doses deplete hepatic glycogen so a per-4-hour and per-day glucagon budget is mandatory; glucagon masks over-insulinization, so every glucagon delivery must feed back as a reduction in insulin aggressiveness (lower the ranged SMB fraction, raise the IOB ceiling's margin) rather than being treated as free rescue; and the controller must never learn to rely on it. The recommendation is therefore sequenced, not refused: build the subtract-only supervisor (item 2) and the personalization in part C first, because they are the insulin-only path; if a second pod is pursued, start semi-automatic, with the controller proposing a dose only on a 30-minute predicted low and the user confirming with one tap, hard budgets, full logging of every proposal and confirmation, and months of replay validation on the user's own data before any proposal is enacted without confirmation. That mirrors how the field itself moved: Inreda's one-year data came after a two-week RCT and a home pilot with no severe events during closed loop ([Blauw et al. 2016](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/299342450_Performance_and_safety_of_an_integrated_bihormonal_artificial_pancreas_for_fully_automated_glucose_control_at_home)).

### C. What can be learned from this user's own data with no meal entry

Trio today learns almost nothing, and what it learns is entangled with the user's static profile. The only live adaptation is one autosens scalar clamped to 0.7–1.2, computed from the median non-meal deviation over 8 and 24 hours (`trio-oref/lib/determine-basal/autosens.js:316-414`), or Dynamic ISF, which overwrites that ratio with a TDD-and-glucose function and leaves basal unscaled unless `tddAdjBasal` is on (`determine-basal.js:407-455, 663-682`). CR, basal shape, DIA and peak time, absorption constants and exercise response are never learned; autotune is dead code (`OpenAPS.swift:804, 817`). The fork's `AdaptiveKalmanFilter`, `BGResidualCalculator`, `ExerciseSensitivityModel` (aerobic +25% ISF peaking at 3 hours and decaying over 24; anaerobic −10% for 1 hour), `DailyStateVector` (Garmin-derived) and `AdaptiveLearning` (EWMA alphas 0.25/0.15/0.07, minimum 5 observations) all run in shadow and feed nothing into oref (`Trio/Sources/APS/SignalProcessing/*.swift`; `APSManager.swift:509-522`); SmartSense's Garmin blend reaches only the bolus-calculator UI (`Trio/Sources/Services/SmartSense/SmartSenseManager.swift:5-16, 101`); and upstream closed the one learned time-of-day-prior proposal as "not planned" ([nightscout/Trio #1516](https://github.com/nightscout/Trio/issues/1516)). The field, by contrast, has normalized carb-free learning: the 780G sets its gains from a trailing 2–6 days of TDD plus fasting-glucose and plasma-insulin estimates ([Enciso Izquierdo 2024](https://www.elsevier.es/en-revista-endocrinologia-diabetes-nutricion-english-ed--413-pdf-download-S2530018024000702)), Omnipod 5 derives its entire automated capacity from a decaying TDI ([Omnipod HCP page](https://www.omnipod.com/hcp/clinical-resources/provider-resources/omnipod-5-algorithm)), the iLet starts from body weight and adapts basal and corrections from the previous 24 hours of glucose ([Beta Bionics guide](https://www.betabionics.com/wp-content/uploads/LA000061_G-Educational-Resource-Guide.pdf)), and DBLG2's "self-learning module ... adjusts algorithm parameters based on estimated glycemia history and insulin delivery history" with meal announcements optional ([Diabeloop](https://www.diabeloop.com/media-press/press-releases/diabeloop-fda-510k-clearance)). None of these needs a carb entry.

The safety design for all of this is the same regardless of row. Learning is slow, daily or weekly, never intra-meal; CamAPS freezes learning during overrides and the first weeks, and the 780G relearns on a 2–6-day window, so Trio should too. Every learned parameter lives in a bounded corridor around the clinician-entered value (autotune's 20%-per-run and autosens-min/max bounds are reasonable starting corridors, `trio-oref/lib/autotune/index.js:238-292`) with bounded step sizes, and the first release is reduce-only: learned values may lower insulin before they are permitted to raise it. Anything that adds insulin is monotone and capped, exactly as Boost constrains its meal model. Every learned value is explainable in the UI with the evidence behind it (the episodes, the nights, the exercise sessions that moved it), and a single toggle reverts to the static profile. The realistic gain from personalization alone is **3–5 TIR points**, consistent with the UVA RCT's 72→77% and the in-silico 2–4 points, which is modest on its own but is also the component that removes the most user work; and none of it can be scored without the replay harness in item 1, which is why that item ranks first.

## Target architecture: an estimator, a learner, a controller and a supervisor, migrated in stages

The end state is a five-layer stack in which oref1 survives as one interchangeable controller behind an independent supervisor, and the fork's shadow pipeline becomes the production estimator. Layer 0 is the unchanged input assembly and enactment shell (`OpenAPS.determineBasal`, `TrioCustomOrefVariables`, `enactDetermination`). Layer 1 is state estimation in Swift: the Kalman BG/velocity/acceleration state, the residual-based disturbance estimator and meal detector, an insulin-specific PK/IOB model, and an observed-absorption estimator with a detector-selected absorption-class library, all publishing a single `StateEstimate` each loop from glucose, insulin, wearable and time inputs alone. Layer 2 is the slow learner: per-time-block basal, ISF and CR with TDD priors and periodic twin refits, learned from detected episodes and the insulin and glucose record rather than from meal entries, writing a learned profile that the controller consumes exactly as it consumes today's user profile. Layer 3 is the controller: predictions and `insulinReq` from the estimate and learned profile, then a monotone sizing function (ranged fraction, TDD-anchored caps). Layer 4 is the supervisor: predictive suspend, velocity gate, IOB ceiling, rolling caps, anti-windup exits, CGM quality gate, each able only to reduce the proposal. Layer 5 is telemetry and replay storage of every input, estimate, proposal and supervisor decision, so the harness can re-run any day.

The migration has four stages, each shippable on its own. **Stage 0 (prerequisite):** finish the rebase onto upstream Trio v1.0.x's Swift oref so the 306 lines of JS divergence are not ported twice, move the meal-window state out of `TrioSettings` into a dedicated store, and build the harness (item 1). **Stage 1 (supervise and resize):** introduce the supervisor (item 2) as a Dosing-Mode-style derived override at the two OpenAPS call sites, the pattern upstream accepted for LGS/Basal Testing ([Trio PR #1506](https://github.com/nightscout/Trio/pull/1506)), then replace sizing (item 3) and the delta lockout (item 4) with the fork's multiplicative stack removed. At the end of Stage 1 the core is still oref1 but its aggression is monotone and bounded, which is the state in which the sushi case gets 1–3 U instead of 0.5 U without a new hypo path. **Stage 2 (estimate and learn):** promote the estimator (items 4, 6, 8) and the learner (items 5, 9, 10) from shadow to live one component at a time, each gated by replay scores; at this point basal/ISF/CR become learned outputs and the one-tap meal signal is an optional prior that the system must perform without. **Stage 3 (decide the core):** with the estimator and supervisor fixed, run a second controller in shadow against oref1 on identical inputs, the obvious candidate being a zone-cost formulation (asymmetric target zone, velocity-weighted correction, diurnal zone such as 80–140 by day and 90–140 overnight) that reuses the same predictions; promote it only if it wins on the replay metrics below for several months. This is the point at which "delete oref1" becomes an evidence-based decision rather than a bet, and the architecture makes it a one-line swap.

Two design rules from the field apply across every layer. Learned or new components are monotone: anything that learns may only reduce insulin unless it is the single designated component allowed to add it (Boost), and user overrides and temporary modes never feed the learner (CamAPS). And every mechanism that adds insulin has a matching, independent mechanism that removes it: ranged fractions pair with the velocity gate, the integrator pairs with its sign reset and clamp, the detector pairs with the Recovering state and FP budget.

## Validation plan: nothing new doses until it beats the replay on hypoglycemia first

Validation runs in four environments with fixed metrics and explicit promotion gates. The metrics, computed identically everywhere, are: TIR 70–180 and TITR 70–140; TBR <70 and <54 with event counts and durations; TAR >180 and >250; LBGI and HBGI; postprandial peak and 3-hour AUC above 140 after each detected or announced meal, since no retrieved commercial source reports this and it is the quantity the user actually wants to move; time from meal onset to return below 180; total daily insulin and automated fraction; suspension count and cumulative zero-temp minutes (the zone-MPC safety proxy); SMB stacking (max rolling 60-minute SMB total); and detector sensitivity, precision, median delay and false positives per day, scored against a reference set of meal episodes built from retrospective glucose-rise labelling (and whatever sparse carb entries exist) rather than against a carb log the user does not keep, reported subject-wise.

**Environment 1, in silico:** simglucose (with the 2026 counter-regulation and activity extension) and T1DMSIM virtual cohorts with unannounced 60–120 g meals, meals plus exercise, missed meals, sensor dropouts and compression lows; this screens for gross failures and gives the FP/day floor, knowing in-silico FP rates run low. **Environment 2, personal twin:** fit a ReplayBG-style twin to the user's own 90-plus days and re-dose counterfactually, which is the only environment that can answer "would the new sizing have fixed the sushi meal without a 3 a.m. low." **Environment 3, replay:** deterministic re-execution of candidate components over the stored Core Data history with the live controller's actual deliveries as the baseline, scoring agreement and disagreement per loop. **Environment 4, shadow:** the component runs live, logs its proposal beside the live decision for at least four weeks and at least 20 detected meal episodes, and is scored on the same metrics. Prediction-model changes additionally get a 30- and 60-minute RMSE check on GlucoBench-style splits, with a planning figure of 15–25 mg/dL at 30 minutes for a personalized model ([GlucoBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05780)).

Promotion gates are ordered safety-first. A component may go live only if, in environments 2–4, TBR <70 and <54, LBGI and max rolling SMB total are non-inferior to the current controller; the meal detector additionally needs ≤0.5 FP/day and ≥80% sensitivity at ≤30-minute median delay on the user's own data; sizing changes additionally need the postprandial AUC improvement to exceed the TBR change at an exchange rate better than STRIVE's +3 TIR for +0.3 TBR. Live rollout is itself staged: first with the supervisor live and the new component in "reduce-only" mode, then with its additive side capped at the previous ceiling, then with the full ceiling, each step held for two weeks with a one-tap kill switch that reverts to Stage 1 behaviour and never to the pre-supervisor fork. Finally, the learner's outputs are reviewed before they are consumed for the first four cycles, then consumed automatically with a bounded per-cycle step, mirroring the 780G's 2–6-day and CamAPS's 1–3-week warm-ups. If the user wants a single number to hold the project to, it is this: the plan is validated when the twin and shadow environments both show TIR at or above the 76% upper edge of today's zero-input band with ≤1% TBR <70 over 90 days with no meal announcements at all, and the live system is then allowed to prove it; 80% without announcements is the stretch target that, on current evidence, only a second hormone has reached.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the question. The user asked whether to delete oref1 for something that detects rises better, doses harder and prevents lows; the evidence says the dominant variable is not the control law at all but the three things wrapped around it, namely an independent subtract-only safety layer, bounded monotone sizing, and slow per-parameter learning, and that every commercial vendor's 2026–2028 roadmap (Omnipod 6, Vivera, Freedom, 3D Intelligence, DBLG2, Liberty) is converging on exactly those wrappers rather than on a new core. The one open-source project that reached the user's target number did so by keeping oref1 and replacing a single decision at a time under a scoring process, and the one architecture that reliably exceeds 80% with zero input dispenses glucagon.

That reframing also sets expectations honestly, and the Medtronic record is the right yardstick because it is the best-resourced attempt at exactly the user's goal. With insulin only and no meal signal at all, the credible ceiling for type 1 today is the 70–76% band bracketed by the 780G's no-bolus days (70.9%, 76.3% on optimal settings) and Vivera's adult feasibility arm (73.8%, with no TBR disclosed), and reaching it requires accepting the pediatric and poorly-controlled-adult floor of ~50–65% (Vivera 64.7% in 12 children with 11.3% of time above 250 mg/dL; CamAPS HX 49.9%). Vivera's own data show what a one-tap signal buys when added to a reactive detector (64.7→67.9% at 1.1 announcements per day, 73.8→~82% with full announcement in adults), and nobody, including Medtronic, has produced a verifiable zero-announcement result at or above 80% in type 1. This user will not announce, so the plain statement of what the evidence supports is this: for an insulin-only type 1 system with no meal input, the ceiling demonstrated today is 70–76%; the subtract-only supervisor, acceleration-gated sizing, the persistent-error integrator and the carb-free personalization in the learning section are the path to push that band upward by the 3–5 points the personalization evidence supports and by whatever the controller changes earn in replay; and a second hormone is the only route that has demonstrably exceeded 80% with zero input, which is why it is sequenced, not dismissed. Which of those is reached will be known, rather than hoped, because the first thing the plan builds is the instrument that measures it.
